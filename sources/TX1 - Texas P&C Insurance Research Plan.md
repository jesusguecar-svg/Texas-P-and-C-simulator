# Research Report: Texas Statutes and Rules Common to Property and Casualty Insurance (TX1)

## 1. Domain Snapshot

The Texas General Lines Property and Casualty Agent Examination (Exam Code InsTX-PC06) is structured around the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) Content Outline effective September 1, 20261. The TX1 domain, titled **Texas Statutes and Rules Common to Property and Casualty Insurance**, represents the core regulatory blueprint for state-specific compliance1.

### Structural Scope and Cross-Domain Boundaries

The TX1 domain regulates the legal environment, licensing requirements, administrative enforcement, unfair trade practices, and agent ethical obligations common to property and casualty insurance in Texas1.

To prevent instructional overlap, the curriculum architecture enforces strict domain boundaries:

* **TX1 Scope**: Administrative powers of the Commissioner of Insurance; definitions of insurers and market status; producer licensing requirements, renewal cycles, and mandatory disclosures; trade practices, marketing conduct, claims settlement timelines, and anti-rebating provisions; agent duties and commission-sharing frameworks1.
* **TX2 Boundary Exclusions**: Line-specific statutes and coverage mandates are strictly assigned to domain TX2. This includes specific statutory requirements for personal auto (Texas Automobile Insurance Plan Association - TAIPA, mandatory liability limits), residential property (Texas Windstorm Insurance Association - TWIA, Fair Access to Insurance Requirements - FAIR Plan), Commercial General Liability (CGL) mandates, Workers' Compensation indemnity/medical benefit schedules, Surplus Lines transaction tax filings/stamping fees (beyond basic producer licensing eligibility), and the Texas Property and Casualty Insurance Guaranty Association1.
* **National Knowledge Boundary Exclusions**: Generic contract law (offer, acceptance, consideration), pure risk concepts, basic property valuation methods (actual cash value, replacement cost), and standardized policy form structures (ISO home and auto forms) are housed in the general-knowledge domain sections1.

## 4. Deep Explanations

### 4.1 Commissioner Authority and Enforcement

The Commissioner of Insurance serves as the chief executive officer of the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI), appointed by the Governor with the advice and consent of the Texas Senate for a two-year term ending on February 1 of odd-numbered years6. The Commissioner possesses broad statutory authority under Texas Insurance Code (TIC) Title 2 to enforce insurance laws, promulgate administrative rules, regulate insurer solvency, and protect consumers against unfair trade practices1.

The enforcement process follows a structured sequence. When TDI receives a consumer complaint or detects a market conduct anomaly, staff initiate an official inquiry under TIC § 38.0011. The target entity must respond in writing within 10 business days1. If the inquiry reveals potential statutory violations, TDI's Enforcement Division conducts a full market conduct or financial examination under TIC § 401.0511. Should formal administrative action be warranted, TDI provides written notice of hearing to the respondent at least 10 days prior to trial7. Contested cases are adjudicated before an Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) at the State Office of Administrative Hearings (SOAH)7. The ALJ issues a Proposal for Decision, based upon which the Commissioner imposes sanctions such as license revocation, cease and desist orders, or administrative penalties up to $25,000 per violation6.

Under TIC § 38.001, the Commissioner or TDI staff may send a written inquiry to any license holder or insurer regarding its business condition or market conduct1. The receipt of a consumer complaint, market analysis anomaly, or routine supervisory monitoring triggers this inquiry1. The recipient must respond in writing within 10 business days from the date the inquiry is received1. Failure to respond constitutes an independent violation of the Insurance Code subject to administrative sanctions1.

Under TIC §§ 401.051–.056 and 521.003–.004, TDI must examine the financial condition of domestic insurers at least once every five years1. Examiners are granted free access to all books, records, accounts, and paper documents maintained by the entity or its agents1. The cost of the examination is assessed directly against the examined insurer1.

Administrative enforcement actions against license holders follow the contested case procedures set forth in the Texas Administrative Procedure Act (APA), Texas Government Code Chapter 2001, and TIC Chapter 827. TDI must provide the respondent with written notice of an administrative hearing not less than 10 days prior to the hearing date7. The notice must state the time, place, statutory legal authority, and specific factual allegations grounding the enforcement action7. Contested case hearings are conducted by ALJs at the independent SOAH7. The ALJ issues a Proposal for Decision (PFD), which is transmitted to the Commissioner for final order issuance7.

The Commissioner possesses dual statutory pathways to halt unlawful insurance operations:

1. **Standard Cease and Desist Order**: Issued under TIC Chapter 83 after formal notice and hearing against any person violating insurance laws or rules8.
2. **Emergency Cease and Desist Order**: Issued *ex parte* under TIC §§ 83.051–.054 without prior notice or hearing if the Commissioner determines that conduct is causing or reasonably expected to cause imminent, irreparable public injury or insolvency9. Under updated 28 TAC § 1.905 and TIC § 83.053, an affected party served with an Emergency Cease and Desist Order has 60 calendar days from the service date to request an administrative hearing to challenge the order9. Upon receiving a timely written request, TDI must set a hearing date9.

Under TIC §§ 82.001–.056 and 4005.102, the Commissioner may impose administrative sanctions for statutory noncompliance6. The maximum administrative penalty is up to $25,000 per violation, or per day of continuing violation6. Statutory criteria evaluated in setting the penalty amount include the seriousness of the violation, economic hazard to the public, history of prior violations, degree of culpability, and demonstrated good-faith efforts to remediate25. License sanctions range from administrative reprimands, probation, and license suspension, to full license revocation7. A person whose license is revoked cannot reapply for a Texas insurance license for 5 years from the effective date of revocation6.

### 4.2 Insurance Entity and Market Definitions

Texas law strictly classifies insurance entities based on domicile, ownership, organizational structure, and authorization status1.

Insurers operating in Texas are categorized across two distinct dimensions: geographical domicile and regulatory authorization status. Geographically, an entity formed in Texas is a domestic insurer1; an entity formed in another U.S. state is a foreign insurer1; and an entity formed outside the United States is an alien insurer1. Regulates through licensing, TDI issues a Certificate of Authority under TIC § 801.051 to admitted insurers1, qualifying their policyholders for Texas Property and Casualty Insurance Guaranty Association protection3. Conversely, nonadmitted insurers lack a Texas Certificate1. While transacting business with unauthorized carriers is generally illegal under TIC § 101.0518, the Texas Surplus Lines Law under Chapter 981 creates a legal exception, allowing licensed surplus lines agents to place difficult-to-insure risks with eligible nonadmitted carriers3. Surplus lines placements are not backed by state guaranty funds3.

Under TIC §§ 801.051–.053, an insurance company cannot lawfully transact insurance in Texas without obtaining a Certificate of Authority issued by the Commissioner1. An admitted or authorized insurer possesses an active Certificate of Authority1. Admitted insurers must file policy forms, rating manuals, and underwriting rules with TDI, and their policyholders are protected by the Texas Property and Casualty Insurance Guaranty Association in the event of insolvency5. A nonadmitted or unauthorized insurer does not hold a Texas Certificate of Authority1. Transacting insurance on behalf of an unauthorized insurer is illegal under TIC § 101.051, subject to severe criminal and civil penalties8. Under TIC Chapter 981, eligible surplus lines insurers may write coverage for high-risk or specialized Texas exposures that admitted markets refuse to insure, provided the placement is arranged through a licensed Texas Surplus Lines Agent3. Surplus lines policies are not backed by the Texas Guaranty Association3.

Under TIC § 982.001, insurers are classified by geographic formation:

1. **Domestic Insurer**: An insurer organized, formed, and incorporated under the laws of the State of Texas1.
2. **Foreign Insurer**: An insurer formed under the laws of another U.S. state, territory, or the District of Columbia1.
3. **Alien Insurer**: An insurer formed under the laws of a jurisdiction outside the United States (e.g., Bermuda, the United Kingdom)1.

Corporate ownership structures dictate organizational governance and profit distribution:

* **Stock Insurer**: Owned by capital stockholders who invest capital, elect the board of directors, and receive profits in the form of stock dividends1. Stock insurers typically issue nonparticipating policies1.
* **Mutual Insurer**: Owned by its policyholders1. Policyholders elect management and may receive policy dividends (participating policies) when the company realizes operational surpluses1.
* **Texas Lloyds Plan**: Under TIC Chapter 941, an unincorporated group of individual underwriters who join together to execute insurance policies through a designated Attorney-in-Fact1. Lloyd's plan members are individually liable for their specified share of underwriting risk1.

Under TIC § 101.051, "transacting insurance" encompasses any of the following acts performed in Texas by direct communication, mail, or electronic media8:

* Direct or indirect solicitation of an insurance contract10.
* Negotiation preliminary to the execution of an insurance contract12.
* Execution, issuance, or delivery of an insurance policy or contract8.
* Collection or receipt of premiums, membership fees, or assessments12.
* Dissemination or inspection of insurance claims12.

### 4.3 Licensing Requirements and Types

Texas law requires individuals and legal entities engaging in property and casualty insurance transactions to hold the specific license mandated for their operational role1.

The licensing framework distinguishes between primary sales producers and specialized operational roles. An individual General Lines Property and Casualty Agent holds broad authority to solicit, negotiate, bind, and service commercial property, liability, personal auto, homeowners, and workers' compensation policies under TIC § 4051.0511. To operate as a corporate agency under TIC § 4001.106, a firm must obtain a business entity license, ensure at least one officer is individually licensed, and maintain financial responsibility via a $250,000 E&O policy or a $25,000 surety bond12. Special provisional types include temporary licenses (valid 90 days for trainees, barring controlled business sales)3 and emergency licenses (valid 90 days upon an agent's death/disability)1. Nonresident producers acquire Texas licenses through home-state reciprocity under Chapter 40561.

Specialized license categories establish strict operational boundaries:

* **General Lines Property and Casualty Agent**: Authorized under TIC § 4051.051 to write commercial property, general liability, personal auto, homeowners, workers' compensation, commercial line auto, inland marine, and fidelity/surety bonds1. Applicants must be at least 18 years old, submit a fingerprint background check, pass the state examination, and pay fees3. Applicants holding the Chartered Property Casualty Underwriter (CPCU) designation are statutorily exempt from taking the prelicensing examination under TIC § 4002.0033.
* **Business Entity (Agency) Licensing**: Corporations, partnerships, and LLCs acting as insurance agencies must obtain a business entity license under TIC §§ 4001.104–.10612. At least one active officer or partner must hold an individual General Lines P&C license12. Agencies must maintain financial proof to satisfy errors and omissions (E&O) claims via an E&O Policy in a minimum amount of $250,000 with a deductible of not more than 10%13, or a Surety Bond in the principal amount of $25,000 executed with TDI13.
* **Nonresident Agent Licensing**: Under TIC Chapter 4056, a nonresident producer licensed in good standing in their home state may obtain a Texas Nonresident Agent license1. Based on national reciprocity principles, Texas waives prelicensing examination and CE requirements for nonresidents whose home state grants reciprocal privileges to Texas producers1.
* **Temporary License**: Under TIC §§ 4001.151–.156, TDI may issue a temporary license valid for up to 90 calendar days (nonrenewable) to expedite producer onboarding prior to passing the state exam1. The applicant must be sponsored by an authorized insurer or licensed agency that verifies enrollment in a 40-hour prelicensing track3. Under TIC § 4001.157, a temporary license holder cannot write controlled business, nor sell insurance to family members, employees, or business associates, and cannot earn commissions on such sales6.
* **Emergency License**: Under TIC § 4051.054, TDI may issue an emergency license to preserve an agency's business continuity upon the death or total disability of a licensed agent1. Valid for 90 days and extendable by TDI for an additional 90 days1.
* **Managing General Agent (MGA)**: Regulated under 28 TAC §§ 19.1201–19.1206 and TIC Chapter 4053, an MGA is a person or entity granted underwriting management, rate-setting, or claims adjustment authority by an insurer3. MGAs handle portfolio administration ("holding the company's pen")15.
* **Surplus Lines Agent**: Regulated under TIC § 981.202, a surplus lines license allows producers to place coverage with eligible nonadmitted insurers3. Applicant must already hold an active Texas General Lines P&C Agent license or MGA license3.
* **Adjuster**: Regulated under TIC Chapter 4101, an adjuster investigates, evaluates, and negotiates settlement of claims on behalf of insurers1. Adjusters cannot solicit or sell insurance policies1.
* **Risk Manager**: Regulated under TIC § 4153.051 and 28 TAC §§ 19.1301–19.1320, a risk manager evaluates risk exposures and provides insurance risk advice for compensation1. A risk manager cannot sell, solicit, or bind insurance policies under this license1.

### 4.4 License Administration and Lifecycle

Producers must comply with continuous regulatory duties to maintain active licensing authority3.

A producer's license is issued on a biennial cycle expiring on the last day of the producer's birth month3. To maintain active status, individual producers must complete 24 hours of continuing education (CE) every two years, including 3 hours of ethics and at least 12 hours of classroom or classroom-equivalent instruction3. Proof of CE completion must be retained for 5 years6. Failure to complete CE results in a fine of $50 per uncompleted hour16. While holding a license establishes producer qualifications, an agent cannot solicit business for a carrier without an active Appointment filed by that carrier under TIC § 4001.2011.

The license administration lifecycle involves specific compliance actions across several operational events:

* **Insurer Appointments**: Holding an agent license grants authority to hold oneself out as a producer, but an agent cannot transact business or bind coverage for a specific insurer without an active Appointment filed by that authorized carrier under TIC §§ 4001.201–.2061. Insurers must file appointment notices with TDI along with nonrefundable statutory appointment fees1. When an appointment is terminated, the insurer must notify TDI immediately1. Termination of an appointment does not revoke the agent's underlying license1.
* **Continuing Education (CE) Rules**: Under TIC §§ 4004.051–.055 and 28 TAC §§ 19.1001–19.1030, licensed producers must complete 24 credit hours of CE per biennial term3. At least 3 credit hours must cover ethics and consumer protection3. At least 50% of hours (12 hours) must be completed in classroom or approved classroom-equivalent formats3. Producers failing to complete CE prior to expiration face an administrative fine of $50 per uncompleted credit hour16. Paying CE fines does not excuse the producer from completing the missing CE hours16. Producers must maintain CE completion certificates for 5 years6.
* **License Renewal and Expiration**: Licenses are renewed biennially upon payment of a $50 renewal fee3. If a license expires, late renewal within 90 days requires paying the $50 fee plus a $25 late penalty3. If expired between 91 days and 1 year, the applicant must file a reinstatement application and pay license fees plus penalties without retaking the exam3. Lapses exceeding 1 year require completing a full new application and retaking the state prelicensing examination3.
* **Mandatory Disclosures and Address Updates**: Under TIC § 4001.252, producers must notify TDI "immediately" of any change in physical business address, mailing address, or legal name16. Under 28 TAC § 19.906, monthly address reporting is enforced17. Under 28 TAC § 1.1302(c), changes to official email addresses must be updated within 10 business days17. Felony convictions must be reported monthly or within 30 days under 28 TAC § 1.5026. Final administrative actions taken by other state regulators must be reported within 30 calendar days under TIC § 4005.1026.

### 4.5 Marketing Practices and Unfair Trade Standards

Texas enforces comprehensive fair marketing standards under TIC Chapters 541, 542, 544, and 7011.

Texas separates marketing conduct into deceptive practices under Chapter 541 and strict claim processing timelines under Chapter 542 Subchapter B (Prompt Payment of Claims Act)1. Under the Prompt Payment Act, an insurer must acknowledge receipt of a claim, commence investigation, and request proof of loss items within 15 calendar days18. Once all requested items are received, the insurer has 15 business days to issue a written notice of acceptance or rejection18. Upon accepting a claim, payment must be remitted within 5 business days18. If an insurer delays payment beyond 60 calendar days after receiving proof of loss, it automatically triggers an 18% annual statutory interest penalty plus reasonable attorney's fees under TIC § 542.06018.

Marketing misconduct and rating standards are strictly governed across several statutory categories:

* **False Advertising**: Under 28 TAC §§ 21.111 and 21.115, publishing or broadcasting advertisements that are deceptive, misrepresent coverage terms, or omit statutory exclusions is prohibited1.
* **Misrepresentation**: Under TIC § 541.051, making false statements regarding policy dividends, financial conditions, policy benefits, or future terms is an unfair trade practice1.
* **Defamation**: Under TIC § 541.053, making or circulating false or maliciously critical statements regarding the financial condition of an insurer or agent intended to cause economic injury is prohibited1.
* **Controlled Business**: Under TIC § 4001.104, a producer license will be refused or revoked if used primarily to write controlled business (insurance covering the agent, family members, or business employer)10. At least 25% of annual premium volume must be generated from the general public (controlled business cannot exceed 75%)10.
* **Rebating**: Under TIC § 541.056 and § 1806.104, offering or paying any rebate of premium, special favor, or valuable consideration not specifically stated in the policy contract as an inducement to buy insurance is illegal2.
* **Unfair Discrimination**: Under TIC § 544.002, charging different rates or refusing coverage based on race, religion, color, national origin, or gender without demonstrating valid actuarial rating differences is prohibited22.
* **Insurance Fraud Reporting**: Under TIC Chapter 701, any person or insurer suspecting insurance fraud must report the matter in writing to the TDI Fraud Unit within 30 calendar days of making the determination23. Reporters acting in good faith receive statutory immunity from civil liability23.
* **Boycott, Coercion, and Intimidation**: Under TIC § 541.054, engaging in acts of boycott, coercion, or intimidation that result in unreasonable restraint of trade or monopoly in the business of insurance is an unfair trade practice1.
* **Rating and Underwriting Practices ("Filings Made Easy")**: Under 28 TAC §§ 5.9340–5.9357, insurers writing property and casualty insurance in Texas must file a complete copy of their underwriting guidelines with TDI at least once every 3 years on or before March 15. Any modification to underwriting guidelines must be filed with TDI not later than 10 days after implementation5.

### 4.6 Agent Duties and Commission Sharing

Producers must comply with statutory limits governing insurance compensation21.

Under TIC § 4005.053, an insurer or agent cannot pay, directly or indirectly, a commission or valuable consideration to any person performing acts of an agent in Texas unless that person holds an active insurance license21.

Statutory exceptions and dual compensation disclosures dictate lawful financial transactions:

* **Deferred Commissions**: Renewal or deferred commissions may be paid to a person who no longer holds an agent license (e.g., a retired agent) provided the person held a valid license at the time the insurance contract was solicited or written21.
* **Referral Fee Exception**: Under TIC § 4001.157 and § 4005.053(c), paying a fee to an unlicensed person for referring a potential customer is lawful only if:
  1. The referral fee is a nominal, fixed dollar amount21;
  2. Payment is not contingent on the purchase or application for insurance21; AND
  3. The unlicensed referrer does not discuss specific policy terms, rates, or coverage conditions14.
* **Client Fee Disclosures and Dual Compensation**: Under TIC § 4005.054, an agent receiving a commission for servicing a client cannot charge an additional client fee for the same service unless full statutory disclosure is made21. Under TIC § 4005.004, if an agent charges a service fee and receives third-party commission, the agent must obtain the client's documented prior written acknowledgment following disclosure of compensation computation methods8.

## 7. Licensing and Enforcement Application Lab

The scenarios below demonstrate how primary statutory principles are applied in examination problem-solving.

### Scenario 1: Selecting the Correct License Type

* **Case Facts**: An independent risk consultant is hired by a commercial construction corporation to analyze property loss exposures and recommend coverage limits. The consultant receives a flat fee of $15,000 for these advisory services. The construction corporation then asks the consultant to execute and bind the property policy directly with an admitted carrier.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 4153.051 (Risk Manager) and TIC § 4051.051 (General Lines P&C Agent)1.
* **Legal Application**: Receiving compensation for analyzing risk exposures requires a Risk Manager License1. However, under TIC § 4153.051, a Risk Manager license does not authorize the binding or effectuation of insurance policies1. To execute and bind the policy, the consultant must hold an active General Lines Property and Casualty Agent License1.
* **Outcome**: The consultant may provide risk analysis under a Risk Manager license, but must hold a General Lines P&C license to bind the coverage. The nearest distractor is assuming a Risk Manager license includes policy sales authority.

### Scenario 2: Unlicensed Activity and Entity Sanctions

* **Case Facts**: An unlicensed corporate agency, "Apex Property Risk LLC," solicits commercial property policies across Texas through its licensed individual officer. The LLC receives $120,000 in commissions directly from carriers without holding a Texas business entity agency license.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 101.051, TIC § 4001.106, and TIC § 82.0528.
* **Legal Application**: Acting as an insurance agency without a corporate license constitutes unauthorized insurance transactions under TIC § 101.0518. Under TIC § 4001.106, corporate entities must be separately licensed and provide proof of financial responsibility ($250,000 E&O or $25,000 bond)12.
* **Outcome & Penalty**: The Commissioner may issue a Cease and Desist Order against the LLC and assess administrative penalties up to $25,000 per violation under TIC § 82.0526. The nearest distractor is assuming individual officer licensure excuses entity licensing requirements.

### Scenario 3: Producer Lacking Required Appointment

* **Case Facts**: An agent holds an active Texas General Lines P&C license. A client requests an umbrella policy. The agent solicits the application and binds coverage with "Lone Star Indemnity," an admitted carrier. Lone Star has not formally appointed the agent with TDI.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC §§ 4001.201–.2061.
* **Legal Application**: A producer license authorizes an individual to hold oneself out as an agent, but the agent cannot bind coverage or act on behalf of a specific insurer without an active Appointment filed with TDI1. Binding coverage without an appointment violates TIC § 4001.201, subjecting both the producer and the carrier to administrative sanctions1.
* **Outcome**: Binding coverage without an active appointment is illegal. The decisive distinction is holding a license versus being appointed.

### Scenario 4: Continuing Education Noncompliance

* **Case Facts**: A licensed agent completes 18 hours of CE (including 3 hours of ethics) prior to her biennial license expiration date, leaving 6 hours uncompleted. She submits her $50 renewal fee on the expiration date.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 4004.051, TIC § 4004.055, and 28 TAC § 19.10033.
* **Legal Application**: The agent failed to complete the mandatory 24 CE hours3.
* **Outcome & Penalty**: TDI assesses an administrative fine of $50 per uncompleted hour (6 hours × $50 = $300 fine)16. Paying the $300 fine does not waive the requirement; the agent must complete the missing 6 CE hours before TDI will renew the license to active status16. The nearest distractor is assuming paying the fine waives missing CE hours.

### Scenario 5: Record Retention Audit

* **Case Facts**: TDI conducts a market conduct examination of an agency's records. The examiners discover that the agency discarded all CE completion records after 2 years and commingled customer premium balances in the agency's general operational operating account.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 4001.255 and 28 TAC § 19.10163.
* **Legal Application**: Under TIC § 4001.255, producers must maintain insurance transaction records separate from non-insurance business6. Under 28 TAC § 19.1016, CE completion certificates must be retained for 5 years6.
* **Outcome**: Commingling fiduciary premium funds and destroying CE records within 2 years violates statutory recordkeeping rules, exposing the agency to administrative fines and license probation6.

### Scenario 6: License Expiration vs. Revocation

* **Case Facts**: Producer A forgot to renew his license, which expired 60 days ago. Producer B had her license revoked by Commissioner order 60 days ago due to premium conversion fraud. Both apply to resume writing insurance.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 4003.006 and TIC § 4005.1053.
* **Legal Application**:
  * **Producer A (Expiration <= 90 Days)**: Can reinstate the license by completing any missing CE, paying the $50 renewal fee, and paying a 50% late renewal penalty ($25) without retaking the exam3.
  * **Producer B (Revocation)**: Is subject to a 5-year statutory bar under TIC § 4005.105 before she can even apply for a new license, which requires complete re-examination and TDI approval6.
* **Outcome**: Expiration allows late renewal within 90 days; revocation enforces a 5-year reapplication bar3.

### Scenario 7: Unreported Contact and Criminal Information

* **Case Facts**: An agent moves his physical agency location on January 1. On February 15, he is convicted of felony financial fraud. On March 1, he updates his mailing address with TDI but fails to report the felony conviction.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 4001.252 and 28 TAC § 1.5026.
* **Legal Application**: The agent violated TIC § 4001.252 by failing to report his physical address change "immediately" / monthly16. Furthermore, failing to report a felony conviction within 30 days / monthly under 28 TAC § 1.502 constitutes an independent ground for mandatory license revocation6.
* **Outcome**: Failure to disclose felony convictions within 30 days results in license revocation6.

### Scenario 8: TDI Inquiry Non-Response

* **Case Facts**: TDI mails a TIC § 38.001 written inquiry regarding a consumer complaint to an agent on June 1. The agent receives it on June 3 but ignores it until June 25 (16 business days later).
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 38.001 and TIC § 82.0511.
* **Legal Application**: TIC § 38.001 requires a written response to TDI inquiries within 10 business days1.
* **Outcome**: Failing to respond by June 17 constitutes a statutory violation, exposing the agent to administrative penalties up to $25,000 regardless of the merits of the underlying consumer complaint1. The nearest distractor is confusing 10 business days with 10 calendar days.

### Scenario 9: Hearing Rights on Emergency Cease and Desist Order

* **Case Facts**: TDI serves an Emergency Cease and Desist Order on an entity on July 1 for selling unauthorized coverage. The entity files a written request for a SOAH hearing on August 15 (45 days post-service). TDI moves to dismiss the request as untimely.
* **Governing Authority**: 28 TAC § 1.905 and TIC § 83.0539.
* **Legal Application**: Under updated 28 TAC § 1.905, an affected party has 60 calendar days from the service date of an Emergency Cease and Desist Order to request an administrative hearing9.
* **Outcome**: Since the request was filed on day 45, it is timely, and TDI's motion to dismiss must be denied9. The decisive distinction is the updated 60-day rule under 28 TAC § 1.905 versus older 30-day rules9.

### Scenario 10: Administrative Penalty Factor Evaluation

* **Case Facts**: An insurer fails to file underwriting guideline updates within 10 days. The error was an isolated system glitch, caused no financial loss to policyholders, and the insurer self-reported the error within 15 days. TDI staff requests a maximum $25,000 penalty.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 82.052 and TIC § 82.0535.
* **Legal Application**: While TIC § 82.052 authorizes penalties up to $25,000 per violation, TIC § 82.053 mandates that penalty amounts must consider gravity, hazard to the public, prior history, and good-faith efforts to remediate8.
* **Outcome**: Based on the lack of public harm and prompt self-reporting, a maximum penalty is unjustified; a nominal fine or reprimand is appropriate8.

### Scenario 11: Unfair Claims Settlement Violations

* **Case Facts**: An adjuster handling a commercial fire loss refuses to pay a valid claim unless the insured agrees to release the carrier from an unrelated pending inland marine claim.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 541.060(a)(4)1.
* **Legal Application**: Compelling an insured to compromise one claim by withholding payment due under another policy section constitutes an unfair claims settlement practice under TIC § 541.0601.
* **Outcome**: The insurer faces Chapter 541 bad-faith liability and administrative sanctions1.

### Scenario 12: Prompt Payment Calculation and 18% Penalty

* **Case Facts**: An insured files a property loss claim on Jan 1. Insurer acknowledges and requests proof of loss on Jan 10 (9 days; compliant)18. Insured submits all requested items on Jan 20. Insurer approves the claim in writing on Feb 1 (8 business days; compliant)18. However, Insurer fails to issue the payment check until April 10 (80 calendar days after proof receipt)18.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC §§ 542.057, 542.058, and 542.06018.
* **Legal Application**: The insurer violated TIC § 542.057 by failing to pay within 5 business days of approval18. Furthermore, because total payment delayed beyond 60 calendar days from receipt of proof of loss items, TIC § 542.060 is triggered18.
* **Outcome & Penalty**: Insurer owes the full claim amount plus 18% annual statutory interest calculated from day 61 through the date of payment, plus reasonable attorney fees18.

### Scenario 13: Misrepresentation vs. False Advertising

* **Case Facts**: Agent X publishes a newspaper advertisement claiming his agency's property policies "guarantee annual 15% dividend returns." Agent Y tells a specific client during a sales meeting that a policy covers flood damage when it explicitly excludes flood.
* **Governing Authority**: 28 TAC § 21.111 and TIC § 541.0511.
* **Legal Application**:
  * **Agent X**: Committed False Advertising under 28 TAC § 21.111 by broadcasting misleading benefit claims to the general public1.
  * **Agent Y**: Committed Misrepresentation under TIC § 541.051 by making false policy coverage statements directly to an applicant1.
* **Outcome**: Media broadcasts constitute false advertising; direct client statements constitute misrepresentation1.

### Scenario 14: Controlled Business Calculation

* **Case Facts**: In a calendar year, an agent generates $100,000 in total written premium. $80,000 of the premium is derived from property policies covering her spouse's commercial real estate business, and $20,000 is derived from policies sold to the general public.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 4001.10410.
* **Legal Application**: TIC § 4001.104 requires that at least 25% of total annual premium volume must be derived from persons other than the applicant and non-controlled property10. Here, controlled business represents 80% ($80,000 / $100,000), leaving only 20% from the general public10.
* **Outcome**: Because public business falls below the statutory 25% threshold, TDI will revoke or refuse to renew the agent's license10.

### Scenario 15: Rebating vs. Lawful Promotional Items

* **Case Facts**: Agent A offers a prospective commercial client a $500 cash rebate if he purchases a property policy. Agent B hands out promotional ink pens valued at $5 featuring the agency logo to prospective clients at a trade show.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 541.056 and TDI Anti-Rebating Guidelines2.
* **Legal Application**:
  * **Agent A**: Committed illegal Rebating under TIC § 541.056 by offering valuable consideration not specified in the policy contract to induce purchase2.
  * **Agent B**: Conducted lawful promotional advertising. Small promotional items of nominal value bearing agency branding do not constitute an illegal rebate2.
* **Outcome**: Cash inducements are illegal rebates; branded promotional gifts of nominal value are lawful2.

### Scenario 16: Prohibited Discrimination vs. Lawful Risk Classification

* **Case Facts**: Insurer A charges female commercial property owners 20% higher rates than male owners solely based on gender without statistical proof. Insurer B charges a commercial property owner 30% higher rates because the building stores flammable liquids.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 544.002 and TIC § 2251.00522.
* **Legal Application**:
  * **Insurer A**: Engaged in Prohibited Discrimination under TIC § 544.002 by applying rate differentials based on gender without actuarial justification22.
  * **Insurer B**: Applied Lawful Risk Classification based on demonstrably higher physical loss hazards22.
* **Outcome**: Gender bias without actuarial data is illegal discrimination; hazard-based rating is lawful risk classification22.

### Scenario 17: Mandatory Fraud Reporting

* **Case Facts**: A claims adjuster reviews property loss photos and discovers undeniable digital evidence that the policyholder staged a fake fire loss to collect $200,000. The adjuster denies the claim on day 5. On day 45, the insurer reports the fraud to local police but does not report it to TDI.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 701.05123.
* **Legal Application**: TIC § 701.051 mandates that any person or insurer who reasonably suspects or determines that insurance fraud has occurred must report it in writing to the TDI Fraud Unit not later than the 30th day after making the determination23.
* **Outcome**: Reporting to local police does not fulfill the statutory duty to report to TDI within 30 days23.

### Scenario 18: Unlawful Coercion and Tying

* **Case Facts**: A bank loan officer informs a commercial borrower that her loan application will be approved only if she purchases commercial property insurance through the bank's affiliated agency subsidiary.
* **Governing Authority**: TIC § 541.0541.
* **Legal Application**: Requiring a borrower to purchase insurance through a specific agency as a condition precedent to credit approval constitutes illegal Coercion and Boycott under TIC § 541.054 and TIC § 4001.104(b)1.
* **Outcome**: Policy tying as a loan condition is illegal coercion1.

## 11. Exam Traps

1. **Confusing Insurer Certificate of Authority with Agent License**: Candidates often mistake an insurer's operating permit for a producer license. Resolving Fact: Certificates of Authority are issued exclusively to insurance companies under TIC § 801.051 to authorize transacting business, whereas licenses are issued to individual agents or agencies under TIC Chapter 40011. Rule ID: COA-801.
2. **Treating Nonadmitted Surplus Lines Coverage as Automatically Illegal**: Candidates assume writing with a nonadmitted carrier is always an illegal transaction. Resolving Fact: Placements with eligible nonadmitted carriers arranged through a licensed Texas Surplus Lines Agent under TIC Chapter 981 are lawful statutory exceptions3. Rule ID: UNAUTH-101.
3. **Reversing Domestic, Foreign, and Alien Domicile Definitions**: Candidates confuse foreign state formation with international formation. Resolving Fact: Domestic = Texas; Foreign = another U.S. state; Alien = outside the United States1. Rule ID: DOM-982.
4. **Confusing Stock and Mutual Ownership Structures**: Candidates mix up dividend rights and ownership. Resolving Fact: Stock insurers are owned by capital stockholders (issuing nonparticipating policies), whereas Mutual insurers are owned by policyholders (issuing participating policies eligible for policy dividends)1. Rule ID: STK-547.
5. **Treating Texas Lloyds Plans as Individual Agents**: Candidates assume Lloyd's underwriters are individual producer producers. Resolving Fact: A Texas Lloyds plan is an unincorporated insurance entity operating through an Attorney-in-Fact under TIC Chapter 941, not an individual agent producer1. Rule ID: LLD-941.
6. **Assuming a Producer License Authorizes Acting for Any Insurer Without an Appointment**: Candidates believe holding a license allows binding coverage immediately for any carrier. Resolving Fact: A licensed agent cannot bind coverage or act for a specific insurer without an active Appointment filed by that carrier with TDI under TIC § 4001.2011. Rule ID: APT-4001.
7. **Confusing Appointment Termination with License Revocation**: Candidates assume losing a carrier appointment revokes the agent's license. Resolving Fact: Canceling an appointment terminates representation for that specific carrier but leaves the underlying agent license active1. Rule ID: TRM-4001.
8. **Applying One CE Rule to Every License**: Candidates apply general lines CE rules universally. Resolving Fact: Continuing education rules vary by license type; General Lines P&C requires 24 hours (3 ethics, 12 classroom) biennially3. Rule ID: CE-4004.
9. **Confusing License Expiration with Revocation**: Candidates treat non-renewal and disciplinary revocation as identical lapses. Resolving Fact: Expiration (<= 90 days) allows reinstatement with late fees/CE; Revocation is a disciplinary sanction enforcing a 5-year bar to reapplication under TIC § 4005.1053. Rule ID: EXP-4003.
10. **Calculating Statutory Notification Periods from the Wrong Triggering Event**: Candidates confuse calendar days with business days or miscalculate start dates. Resolving Fact: TDI § 38.001 inquiry responses require 10 business days; Ch. 542 claim decisions require 15 business days post-proof receipt; Emergency C&D appeals require 60 calendar days post-service1. Rule ID: INVST-38.
11. **Treating Chapter 541 and Chapter 542 as Interchangeable**: Candidates merge deceptive trade practices with prompt payment rules. Resolving Fact: Chapter 541 addresses deceptive trade practices and misrepresentations; Chapter 542 Subchapter B enforces strict processing deadlines and an 18% statutory penalty1. Rule ID: CLM-541.
12. **Confusing Misrepresentation, False Advertising, Defamation, and Fraud**: Candidates mix up marketing trade violations. Resolving Fact: False advertising targets public media broadcasts; misrepresentation involves direct false policy statements to insureds; defamation involves malicious false statements injuring an entity's financial reputation; fraud involves intentional deceptive monetary claims1. Rule ID: MIS-541.
13. **Treating Every Benefit to an Applicant as an Unlawful Rebate**: Candidates assume all promotional gifts are illegal inducements. Resolving Fact: Anti-rebating statutes prohibit valuable consideration not specified in the policy, but nominal branded promotional items (e.g., pens, notepads) are lawful2. Rule ID: REB-541.
14. **Confusing Commission Sharing with Rebating**: Candidates view splitting commissions between licensed agents as rebating. Resolving Fact: Commission sharing between active licensed agents is lawful; rebating involves giving an unstated benefit or premium return directly to the buyer2. Rule ID: CSH-4005.
15. **Treating All Risk Classification as Prohibited Discrimination**: Candidates assume charging different rates to different risks is illegal. Resolving Fact: Charging different rates based on demonstrably higher physical loss hazards and actuarial proof is lawful risk classification22. Rule ID: DSC-544.
16. **Overlooking Exceptions to Controlled Business Restrictions**: Candidates assume writing any family policy is illegal. Resolving Fact: Controlled business is permitted up to 75% of total annual premium volume; noncompliance occurs only when controlled business exceeds 75% (public business falls below 25%)10. Rule ID: CTL-4001.
17. **Using Obsolete Statutes or Anti-Rebating Summaries**: Candidates rely on outdated study materials with repealed rules. Resolving Fact: Current 2026 baseline rules enforce updated emergency C&D hearing timelines (60 days under 28 TAC § 1.905) and current email notification rules (10 business days under 28 TAC § 1.1302(c))9. Rule ID: AUTH-003.
18. **Trusting a Malformed Outline Citation Without Validation**: Candidates accept malformed citations literally. Resolving Fact: Outline item C.1.e lists "TIC §§ 19.1201–.1206", which is a typographical error for 28 TAC §§ 19.1201–19.1206 (statutory counterpart TIC Chapter 4053)1. Rule ID: AUTH-008.
19. **Importing TX2 Line-Specific Rules into TX1**: Candidates include personal auto liability limits or TWIA rules in general state common topics. Resolving Fact: TX1 strictly covers statutes common to all property and casualty lines; auto, TWIA, and workers' comp specifics belong in TX21. Rule ID: AUTH-001.
20. **Assuming Unlicensed Referral Fees Are Always Prohibited**: Candidates believe paying any referral fee to an unlicensed person is illegal. Resolving Fact: Paying a nominal, fixed-dollar referral fee to an unlicensed person is legal provided it is non-contingent on sales and no coverage terms are discussed14. Rule ID: REF-4001.
