## **Commercial General Liability (CGL) Policy Framework**

### **Coverage Trigger Mechanics: Occurrence vs. Claims-Made**

The Commercial General Liability (CGL) program established by the Insurance Services Office (ISO) relies on two primary standard coverage forms: the Occurrence Coverage Form (CG 00 01\) and the Claims-Made Coverage Form (CG 00 02\)5. Both forms afford identical scope regarding the types of damages covered under the insuring agreements, but they fundamentally diverge in the event trigger that determines which policy period responds to a loss5.  
Under the Occurrence Form (CG 00 01), coverage is triggered if the "bodily injury" or "property damage" occurs during the policy period, regardless of when the resulting claim or lawsuit is reported to the insurer5. This creates an open-ended exposure for the insurer, as claims arising from toxic torts, latent construction defects, or historic product exposures can be filed years or decades after policy expiration2.  
Under the Claims-Made Form (CG 00 02), coverage is triggered only if the claim for damages is first made against the insured during the active policy period (or an applicable Extended Reporting Period) and the underlying injury or damage occurred on or after the policy’s established Retroactive Date5. The Retroactive Date is a critical underwriting boundary; any incident occurring prior to this date is strictly excluded, even if the claim is served during the active policy term5.  
When a claims-made policy is cancelled, nonrenewed, or replaced with a policy carrying an advanced retroactive date, coverage gaps emerge for prior acts5. To mitigate this exposure, the CG 00 02 incorporates Extended Reporting Periods (ERPs), commonly known as tail coverage5. The Basic Extended Reporting Period (BERP) is automatically provided without additional premium, providing a 60-day short-tail reporting window for unreported occurrences happening on or after the retroactive date, and a 5-year long-tail window for claims arising from occurrences that were explicitly reported to the insurer in writing within 60 days of policy expiration5. The Supplemental Extended Reporting Period (SERP) is an optional endorsement providing an unlimited, permanent tail coverage window5. The insured must request SERP in writing within 60 days after policy termination and pay an additional one-time premium, typically capped at 200% of the annual premium5.

### **Coverage A: Bodily Injury and Property Damage Liability**

Coverage A obligates the insurer to pay those sums the insured becomes legally obligated to pay as damages because of "bodily injury" or "property damage" caused by an "occurrence" within the "coverage territory"2. Bodily injury is defined as physical bodily harm, sickness, or disease sustained by a person, including death resulting from any of these at any time2. Property damage is defined under two distinct prongs: physical injury to tangible property, including all resulting loss of use of that property; and loss of use of tangible property that is not physically injured2. Purely economic losses or intangible harm, such as lost electronic data, do not satisfy the definition of property damage2. An occurrence is defined as an accident, including continuous or repeated exposure to substantially the same general harmful conditions2.  
The Coverage A Insuring Agreement contains standard exclusions designed to eliminate risks that are uninsurable, business risk exposures, or better addressed under specialized policies2. Key standard exclusions include:

> * **Expected or Intended Injury:** Excludes bodily injury or property damage expected or intended from the standpoint of the insured, excluding reasonable force used to protect persons or property2.  
> * **Contractual Liability:** Excludes liability assumed under contracts, except for liability the insured would have in the absence of the contract, or liability assumed under an "insured contract" such as leases of premises, sidetrack agreements, easement agreements, and elevator maintenance agreements7.  
> * **Liquor Liability:** Excludes coverage for entities in the business of manufacturing, distributing, selling, serving, or furnishing alcoholic beverages if liability arises from contributing to intoxication or serving minors8. Standard non-firearm and non-hospitality businesses possess incidental host liquor coverage under standard CGL8.  
> * **Workers' Compensation and Employer's Liability:** Excludes statutory workers' compensation obligations and bodily injury to employees arising out of and in the course of employment2.  
> * **Pollution:** A broad exclusion eliminating coverage for discharge, dispersal, seepage, migration, release, or escape of pollutants at or from premises owned, rented, or occupied by the insured2. An exception applies to host fire heat, smoke, or fumes7.  
> * **Aircraft, Auto, or Watercraft:** Excludes liability arising from ownership, maintenance, operation, or use of autos, aircraft, or watercraft2. Mobile equipment, such as bulldozers or forklifts, is governed under CGL unless being transported by an auto7.  
> * **Damage to Property (Care, Custody, or Control):** Excludes property damage to property owned, rented, or occupied by the insured, or personal property in the care, custody, or control of the insured7.  
> * **Damage to Your Product and Your Work:** Excludes damage to the named insured's product arising out of the product itself, and damage to the named insured's completed work arising out of any portion of it2. The subcontractor exception restores coverage for damage to the insured's work if the damaged work or the work out of which the damage arises was performed on the insured's behalf by a subcontractor2.

### **Coverage B: Personal and Advertising Injury Liability**

Coverage B shifts protection from physical loss to designated personal torts and commercial offense liability2. It obligates the insurer to defend and indemnify the insured against damages arising out of one or more of seven specific offenses committed in the course of business operations2:

> * False arrest, detention, or imprisonment12.  
> * Malicious prosecution12.  
> * Wrongful eviction, wrongful entry, or invasion of the right of private occupancy12.  
> * Oral or written publication that slanders or libels a person or organization, or disparages goods, products, or services12.  
> * Oral or written publication that violates a person's right of privacy12.  
> * Use of another's advertising idea in an advertisement12.  
> * Infringing upon another's copyright, trade dress, or slogan in an advertisement12.

Coverage B explicitly excludes offenses committed by insureds operating in media, publishing, or advertising businesses, as well as intentional breach of contract, knowingly false publication, and infringement committed with knowledge of illegal conduct12.

### **Coverage C: Medical Payments and Supplementary Payments**

Coverage C provides direct, first-party style medical expense reimbursement to third parties injured on the insured's premises or due to the insured's operations, without regard to legal fault or negligence2. Medical expenses must be incurred and reported to the insurer within one year of the accident date12. Exclusions include injuries to any insured, hired workers, tenant employees, or injuries covered under workers' compensation2.  
Supplementary Payments apply directly to Coverage A and Coverage B liability claims7. The insurer pays these expenses over and above the established policy limits of insurance7. Supplementary payments include all expenses incurred by the insurer, up to $250 for the cost of bail bonds required because of accidents or traffic rule violations, the cost of bonds to release attachments, all reasonable expenses incurred by the insured at the insurer's request including actual loss of earnings up to $250 per day, all court costs taxed against the insured in a lawsuit, and pre-judgment and post-judgment interest awarded against the insured7.

### **CGL Limits of Insurance Structure**

The CGL policy enforces a structural hierarchy of limits that regulates total insurer payout across single occurrences and entire policy terms7.

| Limit Category | Operational Scope and Interaction |
| :---- | :---- |
| **General Aggregate Limit** | The maximum total amount paid under Coverages A, B, and C during the policy period, excluding losses under the Products-Completed Operations Hazard7. |
| **Products-Completed Operations Aggregate Limit** | A separate aggregate limit capping total insurer payouts for bodily injury and property damage occurring away from premises owned or rented by the insured and arising out of the insured's product or completed work2. |
| **Personal & Advertising Injury Limit** | The maximum amount paid under Coverage B for all personal and advertising injury sustained by any one person or organization7. |
| **Each Occurrence Limit** | The maximum amount paid under Coverage A and Coverage C combined for all bodily injury, property damage, and medical expenses arising out of any single occurrence2. Subject to the applicable Aggregate Limit7. |
| **Damage to Premises Rented to You Limit** | A sublimit under Coverage A for property damage caused by fire to a single premises rented to or temporarily occupied by the named insured with permission13. |
| **Medical Expense Limit** | A per-person sublimit under Coverage C for medical expenses sustained by one person in a single accident2. |

## **Businessowners Policy (BOP) Casualty Coverages**

### **Eligibility Framework and Operational Criteria**

The ISO Businessowners Policy (BOP) form BP 00 03 provides a broad package policy for small to mid-sized commercial enterprises4. The policy integrates commercial property and general liability coverage into a single standardized contract4. Under ISO eligibility rules, a risk must meet strict square footage and annual gross sales limitations to qualify for a BOP4:

> * **Gross Sales Limitation:** Maximum of $6,000,000 in annual gross sales per location4.  
> * **Square Footage Limitation:** Maximum of 35,000 total square feet occupied per building or location4.

| Eligible Business Category | Specific BOP Eligibility Parameters & Restrictions |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Apartment Buildings & Residential Condos** | Eligible without square footage limitations; commercial incidental occupancies permitted up to specified square footage limits4. |
| **Office Buildings** | Eligible up to 6 stories in height and 100,000 total square feet per building4. |
| **Trade Contractors** | Eligible provided annual payroll does not exceed $300,000, exterior work is restricted to 3 stories in height, and subcontracted work does not exceed 10% of gross revenue4. |
| **Restaurants (Limited/Full Service)** | Eligible subject to seating and layout restrictions; limited cooking, fast food, and fine dining qualify provided square footage does not exceed 7,500 square feet4. |
| **Ineligible Risks** | Auto repair or service shops (unless endorsed under specialized ISO BOP auto service revisions), manufacturing operations, banks, places of amusement, and large commercial risks4. |

### **Casualty Coverage Structure in BP 00 03 vs. Monoline CGL**

The liability section of the Businessowners Coverage Form (BP 00 03\) mirrors the primary insuring agreements of the standard CGL occurrence form, covering premises and operations, products and completed operations, personal and advertising injury, and medical payments4. However, the policy architecture presents structural differences4.  
While a monoline CGL policy contains a separate Personal and Advertising Injury limit that operates independently from the Each Occurrence limit, the standard BOP enforces a single combined Liability and Medical Expenses limit13. This single limit applies to all bodily injury, property damage, and personal and advertising injury claims arising out of any single occurrence or offense13.  
For micro-enterprises, ISO provides the Micro-Businessowners Coverage Form (BP 00 04\)15. Designed for businesses with up to four employees and annual sales not exceeding $500,000, the BP 00 04 eliminates building coverage structures, focusing primarily on Commercial General Liability accompanied by basic Business Personal Property limits15.

## **Commercial Auto Liability and Endorsements**

### **Business Auto Coverage Form (CA 00 01\) and Symbol System**

The Business Auto Coverage Form (CA 00 01\) is used to insure commercial auto loss exposures for business entities. Central to the Business Auto Policy (BAP) is the Numerical Covered Auto Designation Symbol System, which defines how coverage applies across owned, hired, and non-owned vehicles6.

| Symbol | Covered Auto Scope and Operational Description |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Symbol 1** | Any Auto (Broadest designation; applies to Liability Coverage only). |
| **Symbol 2** | Owned Autos Only. |
| **Symbol 3** | Owned Private Passenger Autos Only. |
| **Symbol 4** | Owned Autos Other Than Private Passenger Autos. |
| **Symbol 5** | Owned Autos Subject to No-Fault Benefits. |
| **Symbol 6** | Owned Autos Subject to Compulsory Uninsured Motorists Law. |
| **Symbol 7** | Specifically Described Autos (Applies only to vehicles listed in the Schedule). |
| **Symbol 8** | Hired Autos Only (Vehicles rented, leased, hired, or borrowed by the insured). |
| **Symbol 9** | Non-Owned Autos Only (Vehicles owned by employees used in the insured's business). |
| **Symbol 19** | Mobile Equipment Subject to Compulsory Auto Land Vehicle Laws. |

### **Form CA 99 10: Drive Other Car (DOC) Broadened Coverage**

The Drive Other Car Coverage — Broadened Coverage for Named Individuals endorsement (CA 99 10\) resolves a critical personal liability gap created in corporate executive environments6.  
When a corporation furnishes a company vehicle to an executive officer or key employee for both business and personal use, that individual frequently sells their personally owned vehicle and cancels their Personal Auto Policy (PAP)6. Because the executive no longer maintains a PAP, they forfeit non-owned auto coverage6. If that executive or their resident spouse subsequently borrows a vehicle, rents an automobile on vacation, or test-drives a vehicle for personal reasons, they face uninsured third-party liability exposures because the baseline Business Auto Policy only covers non-owned vehicles driven for business purposes6.  
Attaching ISO endorsement CA 99 10 to the commercial auto policy closes this exposure gap by extending PAP-like protections to the scheduled executive and resident spouse6. The endorsement modifies the commercial policy definitions to provide non-owned auto coverage while driving vehicles for personal, non-business use6. Coverage applies only to the specific individual named in the endorsement schedule and their resident spouse, excluding other household members unless specifically scheduled19. Available coverages under CA 99 10 include Liability, Auto Medical Payments, Uninsured Motorists (UM), Underinsured Motorists (UIM), Comprehensive, and Collision physical damage6. Coverage applies only if a specific premium entry is shown for that coverage and individual in the endorsement schedule6. Crucially, CA 99 10 excludes any vehicle owned by the scheduled individual or household members, as well as vehicles operated during auto business operations such as selling, repairing, or servicing autos17.

### **Endorsement Comparative Analysis: CA 99 10 vs. CA 99 17 vs. CA 99 33**

Agents evaluating commercial auto risks must distinguish CA 99 10 from other standard commercial auto endorsements6.

| Endorsement Title | Form Code | Primary Target Risk / Application | Key Distinctions & Structural Mechanics |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Drive Other Car Coverage** | **CA 99 10** | Executives or employees furnished a company car who do not own a personal vehicle or carry a PAP6. | Extends personal auto-like liability, medical payments, UM/UIM, and physical damage to scheduled individuals and resident spouses while driving non-owned personal vehicles6. |
| **Individual Named Insured** | **CA 99 17** | Sole proprietors who insure all personal and commercial vehicles on a Business Auto Policy6. | Re-defines BAP coverage to treat the individual sole proprietor as a PAP insured, automatically covering resident family members for private passenger auto use6. |
| **Employees as Insureds** | **CA 99 33** | Commercial enterprises whose employees utilize their personal vehicles for company business23. | Amends the "Who Is An Insured" provision to protect employees from personal liability while operating personal vehicles on behalf of the business23. |

## **Commercial Crime Insurance Framework**

### **Coverage Triggers: Loss Sustained vs. Discovery Basis**

Commercial crime insurance protects business entities against loss of money, securities, and tangible property resulting from criminal acts such as employee theft, robbery, forgery, or safe burglary3. Standard crime coverage forms written by ISO include the Commercial Crime Coverage Form (Loss Sustained Basis: CR 00 21\) and the Commercial Crime Coverage Form (Discovery Basis: CR 00 22\)3.  
The Loss Sustained Form (CR 00 21\) covers losses that occur during the policy period and are discovered either during the policy period or within a specified post-expiration discovery window, which is typically one year3. Conversely, the Discovery Form (CR 00 22\) covers losses that are discovered by the insured during the active policy period, regardless of when the underlying criminal act occurred, provided the act occurred on or after a specified retroactive date3.

### **The Seven Primary Crime Insuring Agreements**

Standard commercial crime policies allow insureds to select from seven core primary insuring agreements3:

> 1. **Employee Theft:** Covers loss of or damage to money, securities, and other property resulting directly from theft committed by an employee, whether acting alone or in collusion with others3.  
> 2. **Forgery or Alteration:** Covers financial loss resulting directly from forgery or alteration of checks, drafts, promissory notes, or similar written instruments issued by the insured3.  
> 3. **Inside the Premises — Theft of Money and Securities:** Covers loss of money and securities inside the premises or banking premises resulting directly from theft, disappearance, or destruction, as well as structural damage to the premises caused during an attempted theft3.  
> 4. **Inside the Premises — Robbery or Safe Burglary of Other Property:** Covers direct loss of or damage to tangible property, other than money and securities, inside the premises resulting from actual or attempted robbery of a custodian, or safe burglary3.  
> 5. **Outside the Premises:** Covers loss of money and securities outside the premises while in the care and custody of a messenger or an armored motor vehicle company, resulting directly from theft, disappearance, or destruction3.  
> 6. **Computer Fraud:** Covers direct loss of or damage to money, securities, and other property resulting from the use of any computer to fraudulently cause a transfer of property from inside the premises or banking premises to a person or place outside the premises3.  
> 7. **Funds Transfer Fraud:** Covers loss resulting directly from fraudulent instructions issued to a financial institution directing the transfer, payment, or delivery of funds from the insured's transfer account3.

### **Statutory Terminology and Definitions**

Understanding precise legal definitions is mandatory for crime insurance evaluation:

> * **Theft:** Any unlawful taking of property to the deprivation of the insured, encompassing burglary, robbery, and larceny3.  
> * **Robbery:** The unlawful taking of property from the care and custody of a person by one who has caused or threatened to cause that person bodily harm, or committed an obviously unlawful act witnessed by that person3.  
> * **Burglary:** The unlawful taking of property from within a locked premises, building, or safe, where there are visible marks of forcible entry or exit upon the exterior boundaries3.  
> * **Custodian:** The named insured, any partner, officer, or employee while having care and custody of property inside the premises, excluding a watchperson or janitor3.  
> * **Messenger:** The named insured, any partner, officer, or employee while having care and custody of property outside the premises3.

## **Workers' Compensation and Employers Liability Insurance**

### **The Texas Elective Workers' Compensation System**

Texas operates under an elective workers' compensation framework governed by Title 5 of the Texas Labor Code1. Unlike most states where workers' compensation coverage is mandatory for private employers, Texas allows private commercial employers to elect whether or not to carry statutory workers' compensation insurance1.  
Employers who elect to purchase statutory workers' compensation insurance or obtain approved self-insurance certification are classified as subscribers1. In exchange for providing statutory medical, income, and death benefits to injured workers without regard to fault, subscribers receive statutory immunity from common-law personal injury lawsuits brought by employees for workplace injuries1.  
Employers who elect not to purchase statutory workers' compensation insurance are classified as non-subscribers1. Non-subscribers forfeit key legal common-law defenses if sued by an injured worker for a workplace injury1. Under Texas Labor Code Title 5, non-subscribers cannot defend against an employee lawsuit by claiming that the employee was contributorily negligent, that the injury was caused by the negligence of a fellow employee (the fellow-servant rule), or that the employee assumed the risk of injury associated with the employment1.

### **Standard Workers' Compensation Policy Structure**

The standard Workers' Compensation and Employers Liability Policy issued by the National Council on Compensation Insurance (NCCI) contains three operational coverage components1.

| Policy Component | Scope of Coverage & Statutory Mechanics |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Part One: Workers' Compensation** | Pays all statutory medical, income, burial, and death benefits required under state workers' compensation law (Texas Labor Code Title 5\)1. Part One carries no specific dollar limit; the insurer pays full statutory requirements1. |
| **Part Two: Employers Liability** | Protects the employer against common-law liability suits brought by employees for work-related injuries not covered under statutory workers' compensation1. Standard minimum limits enforce $100,000 Bodily Injury by Accident (Each Accident), $500,000 Bodily Injury by Disease (Policy Limit), and $100,000 Bodily Injury by Disease (Each Employee). |
| **Part Three: Other States Insurance** | Extends statutory coverage to operations commencing in non-scheduled states during the policy term, provided those states are listed in Item 3.C of the Declarations page. |

### **Texas Residual Market and Insurance Guaranty Mechanisms**

The Texas Medical Liability Insurance Association, governed under Texas Insurance Code Chapter 2203, establishes joint underwriting mechanisms to provide liability insurance to healthcare providers unable to secure coverage in the voluntary market1.  
The Texas Property and Casualty Insurance Guaranty Association (TPCIGA), governed under Texas Insurance Code Chapter 462, functions as a statutory safety net to pay covered claims for policyholders and third-party claimants when a member property and casualty insurance company becomes insolvent1. Statutory limits apply to guaranty payouts, capping covered property and casualty claims at $300,000 per claim, while paying full statutory obligations for workers' compensation claims1.

## **Specialty Liability Lines and Surety Bonds**

### **Specialty Commercial Liability Lines**

Specialty liability lines address specific operational exposures excluded under general liability policies5:

> * **Liquor Liability (Form CG 00 33):** Provides coverage for bodily injury or property damage for which an insured may be held liable by reason of causing or contributing to the intoxication of any person, or serving alcohol to minors or intoxicated individuals10. Required for commercial entities that manufacture, distribute, sell, or serve alcoholic beverages8.  
> * **Professional Liability / Errors & Omissions (E\&O):** Protects professionals such as insurance agents, attorneys, architects, and engineers against financial loss resulting from negligent acts, errors, or omissions in rendering professional services2. Written primarily on a claims-made basis5.  
> * **Directors and Officers (D\&O) Liability:** Protects corporate directors and officers against personal financial losses resulting from lawsuits brought by shareholders, employees, or regulatory bodies alleging wrongful acts, misstatements, or breaches of fiduciary duty.  
> * **Employment-Related Practices Liability Insurance (EPLI):** Protects employers against claims alleging employment discrimination, sexual harassment, wrongful termination, retaliation, and workplace torts12.

### **Commercial Umbrella Liability vs. Excess Liability Policies**

Commercial entities utilize secondary liability layers to protect against catastrophic losses that exhaust primary underlying policy limits25. While a basic Excess Liability policy purely increases the dollar limits of existing underlying policies without broadening scope, a Commercial Umbrella Liability Policy (such as ISO form CU 00 01\) provides both excess limits and broader coverage mechanics25.  
An umbrella policy provides excess financial indemnity over underlying CGL, Business Auto, and Employers Liability policies25. Additionally, umbrella policies fill coverage gaps by insuring certain risks excluded under primary policies, subject to a Self-Insured Retention (SIR) paid by the insured25. Umbrella policies also incorporate drop-down provisions, functioning as primary insurance if underlying aggregate limits are fully exhausted by paid claims25.

### **Surety Bonding Architecture**

Surety bonding is a legal financial guarantee instrument, not an insurance policy. While casualty insurance is a two-party risk-transfer contract where the insurer anticipates losses across a risk pool, suretyship is a three-party financial contract where the surety guarantees to the obligee that the principal will perform a specific obligation. Underwriting operates under a zero-loss expectation, and the principal must execute an indemnity agreement committing to fully reimburse the surety for any paid claims.  
The three parties to a surety bond are:

> * **Principal (Obligor):** The party bound to perform the specified obligation or undertaking.  
> * **Obligee (Beneficiary):** The party protected by the bond, to whom the obligation is owed.  
> * **Surety (Guarantor):** The financial institution guaranteeing the performance of the principal to the obligee.

| Operational Dimension | Casualty Insurance Contract | Surety Bond Contract |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Contractual Parties** | 2 Parties: Insurer and Insured. | 3 Parties: Principal (obligor), Obligee (beneficiary), Surety (guarantor). |
| **Risk Transfer Mechanism** | Risk is transferred from insured to insurer. | Risk remains with principal; surety guarantees financial solvency/performance. |
| **Loss Expectation** | Actuarial pooling anticipates loss occurrences. | Underwriting operates on zero-loss assumption based on financial vetting. |
| **Subrogation & Indemnity** | Insurer subrogates against negligent third parties. | Principal must sign Indemnity Agreement, fully reimbursing surety for any loss. |

Surety bonds fall into three standard classifications:

> 1. **Contract Bonds:** Used extensively in construction to guarantee project completion and vendor payment. Bid bonds guarantee that a contractor awarded a bid will execute the contract and furnish required performance and payment bonds. Performance bonds guarantee that the contractor will complete the project according to contract specifications. Payment bonds guarantee that the contractor will pay specified subcontractors, laborers, and material suppliers. Maintenance bonds guarantee against defective workmanship or faulty materials for a specified period after project completion.  
> 2. **Judicial and Court Bonds:** Required in legal proceedings. Fiduciary bonds guarantee that court-appointed individuals, such as executors, guardians, or trustees, will faithfully perform their duties and account for all property. Court or litigation bonds are required of litigants to protect the opposing party against loss if a legal remedy, such as an attachment, injunction, or appeal, is wrongfully obtained.  
> 3. **License and Permit Bonds:** Required by state statutes, counties, or municipalities as a condition of granting a license or permit to engage in a specific business or trade. They guarantee compliance with applicable regulatory codes and statutory ordinances.
