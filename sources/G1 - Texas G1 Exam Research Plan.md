# **Texas General Lines Property and Casualty Prelicensing Research Packet: Domain G1 — Property Policies**

## **Domain Snapshot**

Domain G1, titled "Property Policies," represents a major structural domain within the Texas General Lines Property and Casualty Agent examination syllabus (Exam Code InsTX-PC06), administered under the oversight of the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI)1. In the controlling examination outline, document \#124401 (effective September 1, 2026), Domain G1 is designated as Domain I within the general-knowledge section1. It accounts for exactly 22 scored questions out of the 100 general-knowledge questions, establishing a 22.0% weight in the general-knowledge portion and approximately 16.92% of the total 130-question scored examination (which consists of 100 general-knowledge items and 30 Texas-specific items, excluding unscored pretest questions)1.  
The curriculum blueprint for Domain G1 mandates expert-level mastery across first-party property contracts, policy architecture, peril triggers, valuation methodologies, time-element coverages, federal insurance programs, and specialized risk endorsements1. Candidates must demonstrate a nuanced understanding of risk eligibility, covered property categories, policy exclusions, loss settlement conditions, and coverage selection across personal lines, commercial lines, inland marine, and residual property markets4.  
Strict curricular boundaries govern Domain G1 in relation to adjacent domains in the prelicensing program:

> * **Domain G2 (Property and Casualty Terms and Related Concepts)**: Focuses on general contractual principles, coinsurance formulas, vacancy and unoccupancy definitions, mortgagor/loss payee rights, subrogation, and policy conditions. Domain G1 applies these concepts to specific policy forms, focusing on eligibility, property categories, peril structures, and direct versus indirect triggers1.  
> * **Domain G3 (Personal Lines Liability and Auto)**: Covers personal liability (Section II of Homeowners contracts), personal auto policies, and umbrella liability. Domain G1 retains sole jurisdiction over first-party personal property contracts, including Homeowners Coverages A through D and Dwelling Coverages A through E1.  
> * **Domain G4 (Commercial Lines Liability)**: Controls Commercial General Liability (CGL), Commercial Auto, Workers' Compensation, and Third-Party Cyber Liability. Domain G1 is restricted to first-party commercial property, Businessowners Policy (BOP) property parts, Builders Risk, Commercial Inland Marine, and First-Party Cyber property and extra expense forms1.  
> * **Domain TX1 (Texas Statutes and Rules Common to All Lines)**: Addresses TDI administrative regulation, agent licensing, and unfair trade practices.  
> * **Domain TX2 (Texas Statutes and Rules Specific to Property and Casualty)**: Controls Texas-specific statutory mandates, including Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA) statutory operations, the Texas Fair Plan, mandatory notice timelines for cancellation and nonrenewal under Texas Insurance Code Chapter 551, and Texas Automobile Insurance Plan Association (TAIPA) rules1. Domain G1 maintains only high-level boundary awareness of coastal windstorm exclusions and residual market availability1.

Where potential conflicts arise between foundational course build charters and the official Texas examination content outline (Document \#124401), the official examination content outline serves as the controlling authority1. All research and simulator specifications in this packet adhere strictly to Document \#1244011.

## **Deep Explanations**

### **Personal Property: Homeowners Forms (HO Program)**

The ISO Homeowners program provides standardized multi-peril insurance packages that bundle first-party property protections (Section I) with third-party personal liability coverages (Section II) into a single policy contract6. Section I property coverage is structured around four primary coverage components:

> * **Coverage A (Dwelling)**: Protects the primary residential structure, attached structures, and permanently installed fixtures8.  
> * **Coverage B (Other Structures)**: Protects detached structures separated from the dwelling by clear space (such as detached garages, storage sheds, or fences), set by default at 10% of the Coverage A limit8.  
> * **Coverage C (Personal Property)**: Protects personal property owned or used by an insured worldwide, set by default at 50% of Coverage A for owner-occupied forms8.  
> * **Coverage D (Loss of Use)**: Reimburses Additional Living Expenses (ALE) and lost Fair Rental Value if a covered loss renders the residence premises uninhabitable, typically set at 30% of Coverage A24.

#### **HO-2 Broad Form (ISO HO 00 02\)**

Form HO-2 is a named-peril package policy designed for owner-occupants of one- to four-family private residential dwellings used exclusively as private residences8. It protects structural interests (Coverages A and B), contents (Coverage C), and loss of use (Coverage D) against sixteen broad named perils11. These perils encompass Fire, Lightning, Windstorm, Hail, Explosion, Riot, Aircraft, Vehicles, Smoke, Vandalism/Malicious Mischief (VMM), Theft, Falling Objects, Weight of Ice/Snow/Sleet, Accidental Discharge/Overflow of Water or Steam, Sudden and Accidental Tearing Apart of Heating/AC Systems, Freezing of Plumbing/Appliances, and Sudden Damage from Artificially Generated Electrical Current26.  
Section I loss settlement settles Coverages A and B on a Replacement Cost Value (RCV) basis, provided the dwelling is insured to at least 80% of its full replacement value at the time of loss8. Personal property under Coverage C is settled on an Actual Cash Value (ACV) basis28. Major policy exclusions include Flood, Earth Movement, Off-Premises Power Failure, War, Nuclear Hazard, Neglect, Intentional Loss, Governmental Action, and freezing of plumbing systems while the home is vacant or unoccupied unless reasonable care was exercised to maintain heat or shut off the water supply8.

#### **HO-3 Special Form (ISO HO 00 03\)**

Form HO-3 is the standard policy choice for owner-occupied single-family and multi-family residential risks8. Its coverage architecture utilizes an open-peril trigger for Coverages A and B, combined with a broad named-peril trigger for Coverage C8. Under open-peril coverage, all direct physical losses to the dwelling and other structures are covered unless explicitly excluded, shifting the burden of proof to the insurer to establish that a specific cause of loss is excluded8. Personal property remains protected against the sixteen broad named perils detailed in the HO-2 form11.  
Loss settlement for Coverages A and B is conducted on an RCV basis subject to the 80% replacement cost condition, whereas Coverage C personal property is settled on an ACV basis8. Major exclusions for the open-peril dwelling coverage include Wear and Tear, Deterioration, Inherent Vice, Smog, Rust, Mold/Wet Rot, Mechanical Breakdown, Agricultural Smudgings, Birds/Vermin/Insects/Rodents, Structural Collapse (except as provided under Additional Coverages), Flood, Earth Movement, and Settling/Shrinking/Expansion of foundations8.

#### **HO-4 Contents Broad Form / Renters (ISO HO 00 04\)**

Form HO-4 is specifically tailored for residential tenants renting an apartment, home, or condominium unit11. Because the tenant holds no real-property ownership interest in the building structure, the form contains **zero Coverage A (Dwelling)** and **zero Coverage B (Other Structures)** limits11. The core policy structure provides Coverage C (Personal Property) and Coverage D (Loss of Use)11. A limited structural extension is incorporated under Section I Additional Coverages to protect Building Additions and Alterations made at the tenant's expense, capped by default at 10% of the Coverage C limit6.  
Coverage C personal property is protected against broad named perils on a worldwide basis and settled on an ACV basis11. Loss of Use protection under Coverage D reimburses additional living expenses incurred while the rented premises are rendered uninhabitable by a covered peril24.

#### **HO-5 Comprehensive Form (ISO HO 00 05\)**

Form HO-5 represents the broadest first-party protection in the Homeowners program, designed for high-value, well-maintained owner-occupied dwellings8. Unlike the HO-3, which restricts personal property to named perils, the HO-5 provides **open-peril coverage across all Section I property parts**, including Coverage A (Dwelling), Coverage B (Other Structures), and Coverage C (Personal Property)8.  
The decisive distinction between HO-3 and HO-5 lies in the treatment of personal property8. Under HO-5, personal property losses caused by accidental misplacement, lost items (mysterious disappearance), or unlisted physical perils are covered unless explicitly excluded7. Coverages A and B are settled on an RCV basis, while Coverage C is settled on an ACV basis in the base form (though routinely endorsed to Replacement Cost)8. Expanded special limits of liability apply to specific categories of personal property, such as jewelry, furs, and silverware22.

#### **HO-6 Unit-Owners Form / Condominium (ISO HO 00 06\)**

Form HO-6 is engineered specifically for owners of condominium units and cooperative residential units4. The property exposures of a condominium unit owner are divided between individually owned personal property and real property interior elements that fall under the owner's responsibility according to the condominium association's bylaws4. The HO-6 provides Coverage C (Personal Property) and Coverage D (Loss of Use)4. Coverage A (Dwelling) is provided with a nominal base limit (historically $1,000 or $5,000, adjustable upward) to insure interior wall coverings, floor coverings, cabinetry, appliances, fixtures, and structural alterations contained within the unit's interior boundaries4. Coverage B (Other Structures) is excluded from the base form but can be added by endorsement19.  
The base HO-6 covers Coverages A and C against broad named perils, with ACV settlement on personal property and RCV settlement on Coverage A real property additions11. Open-peril coverage for Coverage A structural items can be attached via endorsement HO 17 32, and open-peril coverage for Coverage C contents can be attached via endorsement HO 17 3122.

#### **HO-8 Modified Coverage Form (ISO HO 00 08\)**

Form HO-8 addresses older or architecturally unique residential properties where the replacement cost heavily exceeds the market value, or where construction characteristics (such as plaster walls or custom masonry) create a severe moral hazard under standard RCV underwriting11.  
The form covers Coverages A, B, C, and D against a reduced list of basic named perils (Fire, Lightning, Windstorm, Hail, Explosion, Riot, Aircraft, Vehicles, Smoke, VMM, and Theft)11. Theft coverage is restricted to an on-premises limit of $1,000 per occurrence11. Loss settlement for Coverages A and B utilizes **Functional Replacement Cost** or ACV11. Functional replacement cost settles damage using modern, common construction materials and methods (e.g., replacing damaged plaster walls with drywall) rather than replicating obsolete or expensive historical craftsmanship11.

### **Personal Property: Dwelling Policies (DP Program)**

The ISO Dwelling Policy program provides flexible property insurance for residential structures that do not meet Homeowners eligibility guidelines4. Common eligible risks include tenant-occupied rental properties, seasonal dwellings, vacant homes, or dwellings disqualified from Homeowners programs due to claims frequency or physical condition4. Dwelling policies contain five coverage parts:

> * **Coverage A (Dwelling)**: Protects the primary residential structure and attached additions4.  
> * **Coverage B (Other Structures)**: Detached structures (10% of Coverage A)4.  
> * **Coverage C (Personal Property)**: Owner or tenant personal property located on premises4.  
> * **Coverage D (Fair Rental Value)**: Reimburses lost rental income resulting from direct physical damage to rented portions4.  
> * **Coverage E (Additional Living Expense)**: Reimburses temporary living costs (included standard in DP-2 and DP-3; available by endorsement on DP-1)4.

Unlike Homeowners forms, Dwelling policies **exclude personal liability coverage (Section II) by default**, though liability can be attached by endorsement (e.g., DL 24 01 Personal Liability)1.

#### **DP-1 Basic Form (ISO DP 00 01\)**

Form DP-1 is a basic named-peril property policy11. In its unendorsed state, the form covers only three primary perils: Fire, Lightning, and Internal Explosion11. Policyholders can expand protection by electing Extended Coverage (EC) perils (Windstorm, Hail, Explosion, Riot, Aircraft, Vehicles, Smoke, Volcanic Eruption) and Vandalism/Malicious Mischief (VMM) on the Declarations11.  
All losses under DP-1—including building structures under Coverage A and B, as well as personal property under Coverage C—are settled on an **Actual Cash Value (ACV)** basis28. Coverage D (Fair Rental Value) is capped at 10% of Coverage A, but unlike broader forms, payments under Coverage D **reduce the Coverage A limit of liability** rather than serving as an additional amount of insurance.

#### **DP-2 Broad Form (ISO DP 00 02\)**

Form DP-2 expands structural and contents protection to broad named perils, adding Falling Objects, Weight of Ice/Snow/Sleet, Accidental Discharge/Overflow of Water or Steam, Sudden Tearing Apart of Heating/AC Systems, Freezing of Plumbing, and Electrical Arcing to the DP-1 EC/VMM perils26.  
Coverages A and B are settled on an RCV basis, provided the dwelling is insured to at least 80% of its replacement cost at the time of loss8. Personal property under Coverage C remains settled on an ACV basis28. Coverages D (Fair Rental Value) and E (Additional Living Expense) operate as **additional amounts of insurance** (up to 20% of Coverage A combined) and do not reduce the Coverage A limit.

#### **DP-3 Special Form (ISO DP 00 03\)**

Form DP-3 is the most comprehensive policy in the Dwelling line8. It applies an **open-peril trigger for Coverages A and B**, while maintaining a **broad named-peril trigger for Coverage C**8.  
Loss settlement for Coverages A and B is conducted on an RCV basis (subject to the 80% replacement cost condition), while Coverage C personal property is settled on an ACV basis8. DP-3 provides open-peril building protection against risks such as roof leaks from weather events (provided the exterior structure suffers initial damage), damage caused by wild animals, and theft of building materials during construction.

### **Commercial Lines**

#### **Commercial Package Policy (CPP) Architecture**

The Commercial Package Policy (CPP) is a modular insurance policy designed to combine multiple commercial coverage parts into a single insurance contract6. Monoline policies contain only a single coverage part6. To construct a valid CPP, the policy must include:

> 1. **Common Policy Declarations (IL DS 00\)**  
>    \[cite: 14\]  
> 2. **Common Policy Conditions (IL 00 17\)**: Establishes administrative terms regarding Cancellation, Policy Changes, Examination of Books and Records, Inspections and Surveys, Premium Payment obligations (assigned to the First Named Insured), and Transfer of Rights (Assignment)14.  
> 3. **Two or More Commercial Coverage Parts**: Selected from Commercial Property, Commercial General Liability, Commercial Auto, Commercial Crime, Commercial Inland Marine, Equipment Breakdown, or Farm Coverages6.

#### **Commercial Property: Building and Personal Property Coverage Form (CP 00 10\)**

Form CP 00 10 establishes three main categories of covered property at described commercial premises5:

> * **Building Coverage**: Includes the structure, completed additions, permanently installed fixtures (including outdoor fixtures), machinery and equipment, personal property used to service or maintain the building, and additions/alterations/repairs under construction within 100 feet of the premises13.  
> * **Your Business Personal Property (BPP)**: Covers personal property owned by the named insured and used in the business, located inside the building or in the open (or in a vehicle) within 100 feet of the described premises13. Encompasses furniture, fixtures, machinery, equipment, stock, tenant improvements and betterments, and leased personal property for which the insured has a contractual obligation to provide coverage16.  
> * **Personal Property of Others**: Covers property belonging to third parties in the care, custody, or control of the named insured, located inside or within 100 feet of the described premises6.

#### **Commercial Causes of Loss Forms**

Peril breadth under form CP 00 10 is established by attaching one of three standard Causes of Loss forms5:

> * **Basic Form (CP 10 10\)**: Named-peril form covering 11 causes of loss: Fire, Lightning, Explosion, Windstorm/Hail, Smoke, Aircraft/Vehicles, Riot/Civil Commotion, Vandalism, Sprinkler Leakage, Sinkhole Collapse, and Volcanic Action5.  
> * **Broad Form (CP 10 20\)**: Covers all 11 Basic perils plus 3 additional named perils: Falling Objects, Weight of Snow/Ice/Sleet, and Water Damage (accidental discharge/leakage from plumbing/appliances)5. Also includes limited structural Collapse coverage5.  
> * **Special Form (CP 10 30\)**: Open-peril form covering all direct physical loss unless specifically excluded or limited5. Shifts the burden of proof to the insurer8. Major exclusions include Ordinance or Law, Earth Movement, Governmental Action, Nuclear Hazard, Utility Services (off-premises power failure), War, Water (Flood/Sewer Backup), Leakage/Seepage, Wear and Tear, Continuous Discharge, Mechanical Breakdown, and Employee Dishonesty9.

The causes of loss forms progress in coverage breadth from Basic (11 named perils) to Broad (14 named perils plus Collapse) to Special (open perils covering direct physical loss unless specifically excluded)5.

#### **Time-Element Coverage: Business Income and Extra Expense (CP 00 30\)**

Time-element policies protect against financial losses caused by the suspension of business operations following direct physical loss or damage to covered property by a covered peril5.

> * **Business Income**: Reimburses Net Income (Net Profit or Loss before income taxes) that would have been earned, plus continuing normal operating expenses incurred, including payroll5.  
> * **Extra Expense**: Reimburses necessary expenses incurred during the period of restoration that would not have been incurred had no direct physical loss occurred, spent to minimize business interruption and maintain operations at the described premises or a temporary location5.  
> * **Period of Restoration**: Begins **72 hours after** direct physical loss occurs for Business Income coverage (acting as a time deductible) and **immediately** for Extra Expense coverage5. Ends on the date when the damaged property should be repaired, rebuilt, or replaced with reasonable speed and similar quality, or when business operations resume at a new permanent location5.

#### **Equipment Breakdown Protection (EB 00 20\)**

Equipment Breakdown Protection (formerly Boiler and Machinery) addresses operational loss risks specifically excluded under standard Commercial Property Causes of Loss forms (such as CP 10 30, which excludes mechanical breakdown and electrical arcing)9.

> * **Covered Equipment**: Pressure vessels, boilers, electrical equipment, communication systems, air conditioning/refrigeration units, and mechanical equipment9.  
> * **Covered Cause of Loss**: A sudden and accidental physical breakdown of covered equipment causing physical damage that necessitates repair or replacement9. Includes electrical arcing, mechanical breakdown, and pressure vessel explosion or rupture caused by centrifugal force or internal pressure9.  
> * **Key Exclusion**: Wear and tear, gradual deterioration, corrosion, or erosion are **not** equipment breakdowns; the breakdown must be sudden and accidental9.

#### **Businessowners Policy (BOP \- ISO BP 00 03\)**

The Businessowners Policy is a self-contained package policy that combines first-party property, business interruption, and third-party commercial liability coverages for eligible small to medium-sized commercial risks6.

> * **Eligibility Boundaries**: Restricted to businesses occupying no more than 35,000 square feet per location and generating no more than $6,000,000 in annual gross sales6. Eligible classes include retail stores, office buildings (under 6 stories), apartment complexes, small contractors, and limited-cooking restaurants6. Ineligible risks include manufacturing operations, auto repair shops, bars/nightclubs, and financial institutions.  
> * **Standard Built-In Property Features**:  
  * Open-peril property coverage as standard (BP 00 03\)6.  
  * Replacement Cost Valuation standard on buildings and BPP (provided 80% insurance-to-value is maintained)6.  
  * **Business Income and Extra Expense**: Included automatically on an **Actual Loss Sustained (ALS)** basis for up to **12 consecutive months** following a covered loss, **without a stated dollar limit** on the policy Declarations6.  
  * Automatic Building Limit Inflation Guard (typically 8%) and Seasonal Increase in Personal Property limit (typically 25%)6.

#### **Builders Risk Coverage Form (CP 00 20\)**

Builders Risk insurance provides first-party property protection for commercial or residential structures while under construction, alteration, or repair8.

> * **Covered Property**: The structure under construction, foundations, building materials and supplies intended to become a permanent part of the building (located inside or within 100 feet of the premises), temporary structures, scaffolding, and site preparation equipment8.  
> * **Valuation Basis**: Written based on the projected **Completed Value** of the structure.  
> * **Termination of Coverage**: Coverage terminates upon the earliest of: (1) policy expiration; (2) purchaser acceptance; (3) policy cancellation; (4) construction abandonment; (5) 90 days post-completion; or (6) **60 days after** the building is occupied or put to its intended use8.

#### **Cyber First-Party Coverage**

First-party cyber coverage protects organizations against direct financial loss, data loss, and digital business interruption caused by security breaches, ransomware, or network outages.

> * **Coverage Scope**: First-party cyber endorsements cover Data Restoration Expenses (reconstructing corrupted data)9, Cyber Extortion / Ransomware payments9, Digital Business Interruption (lost profit due to system outage), and Incident Response / Customer Notification Expenses9.  
> * **Boundary Control**: Distinguish strictly from **Third-Party Cyber Liability** (assigned to Domain G4), which covers third-party lawsuits resulting from network security breaches or unauthorized release of private data1.

### **Inland Marine**

Inland Marine insurance covers property that is mobile, floating, in transit, or held by bailees7. Most policies operate on an open-peril basis with worldwide territorial limits7.

#### **Personal Articles Floaters (PAF)**

A Personal Articles Floater (written via ISO form PM 00 09 / PM 00 10 or attached to a Homeowners policy via endorsement HO 04 61\) provides open-peril, worldwide coverage for high-value scheduled personal property7.

> * **Nine Standard Classes**: Jewelry, Furs, Cameras, Musical Instruments, Silverware, Golfer's Equipment, Fine Arts, Stamp Collections, and Coin Collections7.  
> * **Key Coverage Characteristics**:  
  * **Open-Peril Basis**: Covers mysterious disappearance, accidental loss, and breakage7.  
  * **No Deductible**: Standard PAF policies apply no deductible to covered losses7.  
  * **Valuation**: Settled on a Standard Loss Settlement basis (lesser of ACV, repair cost, or limit) via PM 00 09, or an **Agreed Value** basis via PM 00 10 / HO 04 60 (paying the scheduled limit in full for total loss)7.  
  * **Pair and Set Clause**: On partial loss to a pair or set, the insurer may repair/replace to restore set value or pay the difference between ACV before and after loss7.  
  * **Newly Acquired Property Extension**: Automatic coverage for newly acquired items in scheduled classes for up to 30 days, limited to 25% of the class limit or $10,000, whichever is less7.

#### **Commercial Property Floaters**

Commercial Inland Marine floaters protect mobile commercial assets across changing job sites and transit routes14:

> * **Contractors Equipment Floater**: Covers mobile heavy machinery, cranes, bulldozers, and tools used by contractors at job sites, in transit, or in storage.  
> * **Installation Floater**: Covers machinery, supplies, and materials in transit, awaiting installation, or during installation at a job site until physical acceptance by the buyer.  
> * **Equipment Dealers Floater**: Covers inventory of mobile agricultural and construction equipment held for sale by dealers.

### **National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)**

#### **Program Purpose & Structure**

Created under the National Flood Insurance Act of 1968 and administered by FEMA, the NFIP enables property owners in participating communities to purchase federally backed flood insurance18. Participating communities must adopt and enforce local floodplain management ordinances18. Policies are issued directly by NFIP Direct or through private insurers via the **Write Your Own (WYO)** program18. Under WYO, private insurers write and service the Standard Flood Insurance Policy (SFIP) in their own names, while FEMA retains 100% of the underwriting liability and receives all collected premiums18.

#### **Standard Flood Insurance Policy (SFIP) Forms**

FEMA promulgates three distinct SFIP policy forms4:

> 1. **Dwelling Form**: Covers 1-to-4 family residential structures and individual residential condominium units4.  
> 2. **General Property Form**: Covers commercial buildings, residential buildings with 5+ units, and non-residential risks4.  
> 3. **Residential Condominium Building Association Policy (RCBAP)**: Issued to residential condominium associations to cover the building structure and common elements18.

#### **Definition of "Flood"**

Under the SFIP, a flood is defined as a general and temporary condition of partial or complete inundation of **two or more acres of normally dry land area** OR of **two or more properties** (at least one of which is the policyholder's property) from28:

> * Overflow of inland or tidal waters28.  
> * Unusual and rapid accumulation or runoff of surface waters from any source28.  
> * Mudflow28.  
> * Collapse or subsidence of land along the shore of a lake or body of water caused by erosion exceeding cyclical levels28.

#### **Statutory Limits & Valuation Provisions**

#### **Waiting Periods & Statutory Exceptions**

A mandatory **30-day waiting period** applies before a newly purchased NFIP policy becomes effective18. Statutory exceptions permitting earlier effective dates include:

> * **Initial Loan Connection (0-Day Waiting Period)**: Flood insurance required in connection with making, increasing, extending, or renewing a loan (effective at loan closing)18.  
> * **Map Revision (1-Day Waiting Period)**: Initial purchase made within 13 months following a FEMA map revision18.  
> * **Post-Disaster Declaration**: When property is affected by flooding on federal land caused by wildfire.

#### **Exclusions & Limitations**

> * **Basement / Below-Ground Limitations**: Basements (any building area having its floor below ground level on all sides) suffer severe coverage restrictions28. Coverage is restricted strictly to structural elements (foundations, utility connections, HVAC, circuit breakers) and specific appliances (washers/dryers, freezers)28. Finished basement walls, drywall, carpeting, furniture, and personal property in basements are completely excluded28.  
> * **No Loss of Use**: The SFIP **does not cover Additional Living Expense (ALE)** or loss of rental income4.  
> * **Increased Cost of Compliance (ICC)**: Provides up to **$30,000** in additional coverage to elevate, demolish, or floodproof a structure that has suffered repetitive or substantial flood damage18.

#### **Private Flood Insurance**

Private flood policies operate outside the NFIP, offering higher limits (exceeding $250k/$500k caps), inclusion of Loss of Use/ALE coverage, shorter waiting periods (0 to 14 days), and broader peril endorsements4.

### **Other Specialty and Residual Property Risks**

#### **Earthquake Coverage**

Standard commercial and personal property forms strictly exclude Earth Movement (earthquake, landslide, mudslide, earth sinking)8.

> * **Mechanism**: Earthquake protection is added via an endorsement (e.g., ISO HO 04 54\) or written as a standalone policy.  
> * **Deductibles**: Utilizes percentage deductibles (typically **2% to 20%** of the Coverage A or building limit) rather than flat dollar amounts.  
> * **Ensuing Loss Clause**: Standard policies state that if an earthquake results in fire, explosion, or glass breakage, the resulting direct damage caused by that ensuing peril is covered under the base policy26.

#### **Mobile Homes**

Mobile homes present unique mobility and structural risks that render them ineligible for standard HO-2 or HO-3 forms37.

> * **Mechanism**: Covered by attaching a Mobilehome Endorsement (MH 04 01\) to a Homeowners form or issuing a specialized Manufactured Housing policy37.  
> * **Key Provisions**: Requires the home to be permanently attached to a foundation and anchored according to safety standards. Incorporates a **Transportation / Collision Coverage Extension**, which covers physical damage while the mobile home is detached and in transit for a specified period (e.g., 30 days).

#### **Watercraft Coverage**

> * **Homeowners Sublimits**: Homeowners policies (HO-3) provide severely restricted property coverage for watercraft—limited to a maximum of **$1,500** total limit, applying only to named perils, and excluding windstorm coverage while the craft is outdoors22.  
> * **Boatowners Policy**: Standalone multi-peril policy covering small pleasure craft. Combines Coverage A (Hull Insurance) on an open-peril, ACV or Agreed Value basis for the boat, motor, trailer, and equipment, with Protection and Indemnity (P\&I) watercraft liability coverage.

#### **Farm Owners Insurance**

Farm operations present a hybrid risk combining residential living with commercial agricultural exposures20.

> * **Structure**: Uses modular forms like FP 00 10 (Farm Property Coverage Form)20:  
  * **Coverage A**: Farm Dwelling20.  
  * **Coverage B**: Appurtenant Private Structures20.  
  * **Coverage C**: Household Personal Property20.  
  * **Coverage D**: Loss of Use20.  
  * **Coverage E**: Scheduled Farm Personal Property (grain, livestock, machinery)20.  
  * **Coverage F**: Unscheduled Farm Personal Property (blanket farm contents)20.  
  * **Coverage G**: Barns, Outbuildings, and Other Farm Structures (silos, fences)20.

#### **Coastal Windstorm and TWIA Boundary**

> * **Exclusion Mechanism**: Insurers in coastal counties exclude the perils of Windstorm and Hail from standard policies using state-approved windstorm exclusion endorsements (e.g., HO 03 12).  
> * **Residual Market (TWIA)**: Property owners in designated Tier 1 coastal counties who are rejected by admitted carriers obtain wind/hail coverage through the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA). Detailed statutory governance of TWIA is deferred to Domain TX21.

## **Coverage-Selection and Application Lab**

### **Scenario 1: Personal Property Form Selection**

> * **Fact Pattern**: A client purchases an architecturally intact 1890 Victorian home. The estimated replacement cost to duplicate the hand-carved woodwork and plaster walls is $1,200,000, but the current market value of the property is only $350,000. The owner wants basic property protection without paying prohibitive replacement cost premiums.  
> * **Controlling Form**: ISO HO 00 08 (Homeowners 8 Modified Coverage Form)11.  
> * **Correct Analysis**: Form HO-8 is specifically designed for properties where replacement cost heavily exceeds market value, creating moral hazard32. It provides functional replacement cost valuation, settling losses using modern, common building materials (such as drywall instead of plaster)11.  
> * **Outcome-Changing Fact**: If the owner insists on receiving exact reproduction materials and open-peril dwelling coverage, the agent must issue an HO-3 or HO-5 with a full replacement cost endorsement, requiring 100% replacement value underwriting8.

### **Scenario 2: Dwelling versus Homeowners Eligibility**

> * **Fact Pattern**: An investor owns a single-family residential house rented to residential tenants. The investor wants to insure the building for replacement cost and protect rental income against fire damage.  
> * **Controlling Form**: ISO DP 00 02 (Dwelling Property 2 Broad Form) or DP 00 03 (Special Form).  
> * **Correct Analysis**: The property is ineligible for a Homeowners policy (HO-2/HO-3) because it is non-owner occupied8. The Dwelling program accepts tenant-occupied rental properties4. Form DP-2 or DP-3 provides replacement cost coverage on the dwelling structure (Coverage A) and incorporates Coverage D (Fair Rental Value) as an additional amount of insurance4.  
> * **Outcome-Changing Fact**: If the owner decides to move into the house as a primary residence, the risk becomes eligible for an HO-3 Special Form, which bundles personal liability coverage automatically1.

### **Scenario 3: CPP versus BOP Packaging**

> * **Fact Pattern**: A local mercantile clothing store occupies a 4,000-square-foot leased space and generates $1,200,000 in annual gross sales. The owner wants open-peril property coverage for inventory, leased equipment, improvements, and 12 months of automatic business interruption coverage.  
> * **Controlling Form**: ISO BP 00 03 (Businessowners Coverage Form)6.  
> * **Correct Analysis**: The risk falls comfortably under BOP eligibility thresholds (\<35,000 sq ft, \<$6M sales)6. The BOP provides open-peril coverage standard on Business Personal Property (BPP) and automatically includes Business Income and Extra Expense on an Actual Loss Sustained basis for 12 months without a stated dollar cap6.  
> * **Outcome-Changing Fact**: If the business expands into a manufacturing facility generating $10,000,000 in annual gross sales, it becomes ineligible for a BOP and must be insured under a Commercial Package Policy (CPP) using form CP 00 10 and CP 00 30 with stated business income limits5.

### **Scenario 4: Direct Loss with Business Income and Extra Expense**

> * **Fact Pattern**: A restaurant suffers a major kitchen fire on Monday at 8:00 AM, destroying cooking equipment and causing structural smoke damage. Repairs require the restaurant to close completely for 30 days. To maintain operations, the restaurant rents a mobile commercial kitchen vehicle starting Wednesday at 8:00 AM at a cost of $5,000. Lost net profit during the month is $20,000, and continuing payroll is $10,000.  
> * **Controlling Authority**: ISO CP 00 30 (Business Income and Extra Expense Coverage Form)5.  
> * **Correct Analysis**: Direct physical loss to covered property by a covered peril (fire) triggers time-element coverages5. Business Income is subject to a 72-hour waiting period before coverage begins5. Lost income during the first 72 hours (Monday 8:00 AM to Thursday 8:00 AM) is excluded. Business income protection pays lost net profits and continuing payroll incurred after the 72-hour mark until the property is repaired5. Extra Expense has no 72-hour waiting period; coverage begins immediately upon physical loss5. The $5,000 mobile kitchen rental expense is fully reimbursable from Wednesday because it minimizes business interruption5.  
> * **Outcome-Changing Fact**: If the closure was caused by an off-premises power failure resulting from a storm 5 miles away, time-element coverage is denied under CP 10 30 unless the Utility Services \- Time Element endorsement (CP 15 45\) is attached9.

### **Scenario 5: Equipment Breakdown Application**

> * **Fact Pattern**: A manufacturing plant's main electrical transformer suffers internal electrical arcing, burning out the central processing unit and shutting down the assembly line. The commercial property carrier denies the claim under form CP 10 30\.  
> * **Controlling Form**: ISO EB 00 20 (Equipment Breakdown Protection Coverage Form)9.  
> * **Correct Analysis**: Form CP 10 30 explicitly excludes loss caused by artificially generated electrical current and mechanical breakdown9. Electrical arcing is the classic trigger for Equipment Breakdown insurance (EB 00 20), which covers the direct damage to the transformer and consequential business interruption9.  
> * **Outcome-Changing Fact**: If the arcing was caused by a lightning strike hitting the building's electrical line outside, the loss is covered under standard Commercial Property Causes of Loss forms (CP 10 10/20/30) as a direct lightning peril, negating the mechanical breakdown exclusion5.

### **Scenario 6: NFIP Waiting Period and Loan Closing**

> * **Fact Pattern**: A home buyer contracts to purchase a home located in a Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA). The mortgage lender requires flood insurance at closing on October 15\. The buyer submits the flood application and full premium on October 10\. A severe flood damages the home on October 16\.  
> * **Controlling Authority**: National Flood Insurance Act; FEMA NFIP Manual Section 218.  
> * **Correct Analysis**: Although the NFIP imposes a standard 30-day waiting period for new policies, an explicit statutory exception applies when flood insurance is purchased in connection with making, increasing, extending, or renewing a loan18. Coverage becomes effective at the exact time of loan closing (October 15), so the October 16 loss is fully covered18.  
> * **Outcome-Changing Fact**: If the buyer purchased the flood policy voluntarily post-closing without any loan modification, the 30-day waiting period applies, leaving the loss uncovered until November 918.

## **Exam Takeaways**

> * **HO-3 versus HO-5 Peril Trigger**: HO-3 provides open-peril dwelling coverage but broad named-peril personal property coverage8. HO-5 provides open-peril coverage on both dwelling and personal property8.  
> * **HO-4 Coverage Scope**: Tenant form HO-4 contains zero Coverage A dwelling and zero Coverage B other structures limits, providing Coverage C contents and Coverage D loss of use11.  
> * **HO-6 Condominium Property**: HO-6 covers interior building additions, alterations, and fixtures under Coverage A with a nominal base limit, alongside Coverage C contents4.  
> * **HO-8 Loss Settlement**: Insures older homes where replacement cost far exceeds market value using **Functional Replacement Cost** valuation11.  
> * **DP-1 Valuation and Perils**: DP-1 basic form settles all loss categories (dwelling and contents) at **Actual Cash Value (ACV)**28. Extended Coverage (EC) and VMM must be specifically added11.  
> * **BOP Business Income Advantage**: BP 00 03 provides automatic Business Income and Extra Expense coverage on an **Actual Loss Sustained (ALS)** basis for **12 consecutive months** without a stated policy dollar cap6.  
> * **BOP Eligibility Caps**: Businessowners policies restrict eligibility to risks with max **35,000 square feet** per location and **$6,000,000** annual gross sales6.  
> * **Commercial Property Locations**: CP 00 10 covers BPP and Property of Others located inside the described building or in the open/vehicle within **100 feet** of the premises13.  
> * **72-Hour Business Income Deductible**: Business Income coverage under form CP 00 30 incorporates a mandatory **72-hour time deductible** before indemnity payments begin5. Extra Expense coverage begins immediately (0 hours)5.  
> * **Equipment Breakdown Carve-Out**: Form CP 10 30 excludes mechanical breakdown and electrical arcing9. Equipment Breakdown form EB 00 20 covers sudden and accidental arcing, mechanical breakdown, and centrifugal explosion9.  
> * **NFIP Statutory Limits**: SFIP Dwelling Form caps building coverage at **$250,000** and personal property at **$100,000**4. General Property Form caps building at **$500,000** and contents at **$500,000**.  
> * **NFIP Waiting Period Exceptions**: Standard NFIP waiting period is 30 days18. Waiting period is **0 days** when required for a lender loan closing18, and **1 day** following a FEMA map revision18.  
> * **NFIP Basement Exclusion**: Basements (below ground on all sides) suffer strict exclusions; finished surfaces, carpet, furniture, and loss of use are entirely excluded4.  
> * **Inland Marine PAF Characteristics**: Personal Articles Floaters cover scheduled valuables worldwide on an open-peril basis with **no deductible**7.

## **Memory Aids**

> * **"OPEN 5"**: Represents the rule that HO-5 provides Open-Peril coverage on both Dwelling AND Personal Property (Coverage C)8. Supported by Rule ID: R-00311. Note that standard policy exclusions (flood, earth movement, war, nuclear) still apply8.  
> * **"BOP 35-6-12"**: Represents the rule that BOP eligibility caps at **35**,000 square feet6, **$6**,000,000 annual gross sales6, and provides **12** months Actual Loss Sustained business income6. Supported by Rule ID: R-0096. Note that insurers may enforce stricter underwriting guidelines for specific risk classes6.  
> * **"100-FOOT RING"**: Represents the rule that BPP and Personal Property of Others under CP 00 10 are covered while situated within 100 feet of the described premises13. Supported by Rule ID: R-0065. Note that property in transit beyond 100 feet requires an Inland Marine floater or transit endorsement14.  
> * **"72-0 TIME SPAN"**: Represents the rule that Business Income has a **72-hour** waiting period5, whereas Extra Expense has a **0-hour** waiting period5. Supported by Rule ID: R-0085. Note that the 72-hour window can be reduced to 24 hours or eliminated by endorsement for an added premium.

## **Exam Traps**

> 1. **Reversed HO-3 / HO-5 Distinctions**:  
   * *Temptation*: Believing HO-3 covers contents on an open-peril basis.  
   * *Resolving Fact*: HO-3 is open peril on dwelling, but **named peril** on contents8. HO-5 is open peril on **both** dwelling and contents8. Supporting Rule ID: R-002, R-0038.  
> 2. **Confusing Renters (HO-4) and Condominium (HO-6) Coverage**:  
   * *Temptation*: Selecting HO-4 for a condo owner because they live inside a multi-unit building.  
   * *Resolving Fact*: Condo owners own interior real property (alterations/fixtures) requiring Coverage A under HO-64. Renters own no real property and require HO-411. Supporting Rule ID: R-0011.  
> 3. **HO versus DP Eligibility Rules**:  
   * *Temptation*: Assigning an HO-3 policy to a landlord renting out a residential home.  
   * *Resolving Fact*: HO forms require **owner-occupancy**8. Tenant-occupied rental homes must be insured under a Dwelling Policy (DP)4. Supporting Rule ID: R-002, R-0058.  
> 4. **Treating Open-Peril Coverage as Unlimited Protection**:  
   * *Temptation*: Assuming open-peril forms (HO-5, CP 10 30\) cover every loss including flood and wear/tear8.  
   * *Resolving Fact*: Open-peril forms exclude named hazards (flood, earth movement, mechanical breakdown, wear/tear)9; "all-risk" is an unapproved misnomer8. Supporting Rule ID: R-003, R-0075.  
> 5. **Confusing CPP and BOP Business Interruption Limits**:  
   * *Temptation*: Applying a 50% coinsurance clause or stated dollar cap to a BOP business income claim.  
   * *Resolving Fact*: BOP business income is written on an **Actual Loss Sustained (ALS) basis for 12 months** with no dollar cap6. CPP CP 00 30 requires stated dollar limits5. Supporting Rule ID: R-008, R-0095.  
> 6. **BPP 100-Foot Boundary Errors**:  
   * *Temptation*: Disallowing BPP coverage for property sitting on a loading dock 20 feet outside the building.  
   * *Resolving Fact*: CP 00 10 explicitly extends BPP coverage to property in the open or in vehicles within **100 feet** of the premises13. Supporting Rule ID: R-0065.  
> 7. **Business Income 72-Hour Waiting Period Misunderstanding**:  
   * *Temptation*: Calculating lost profit reimbursement starting immediately from the minute a fire occurs.  
   * *Resolving Fact*: Business income lost during the **first 72 hours** is excluded as a time deductible5. Supporting Rule ID: R-0085.  
> 8. **Mistaking Equipment Breakdown for Wear and Tear**:  
   * *Temptation*: Claiming Equipment Breakdown coverage for an old motor that slowly burned out from age.  
   * *Resolving Fact*: EB 00 20 covers only **sudden and accidental** physical breakdown; gradual wear and tear is excluded9. Supporting Rule ID: R-0149.  
> 9. **Confusing Inland Marine with Ocean Marine**:  
   * *Temptation*: Requiring an ocean marine policy for cargo being transported via domestic semi-trucks.  
   * *Resolving Fact*: Over-the-land transit and mobile domestic assets fall under **Inland Marine**14. Ocean marine covers ocean vessel hulls and transoceanic cargo. Supporting Rule ID: R-0127.  
> 10. **Using Obsolete NFIP Statutory Limits**:  
    * *Temptation*: Selecting $100,000 or $500,000 as the maximum NFIP residential dwelling limit on a test question.  
    * *Resolving Fact*: Current statutory maximum SFIP limits are **$250,000 Building / $100,000 Contents** for residential dwellings4. Supporting Rule ID: R-01018.  
> 11. **Assuming Flood or Windstorm Coverage is Universal**:  
    * *Temptation*: Believing an HO-3 covers rising water from hurricane storm surge.  
    * *Resolving Fact*: Flood is universally excluded under standard personal/commercial property forms8; flood must be purchased via the NFIP or private flood market4. Supporting Rule ID: R-002, R-0108.  
> 12. **Importing Detailed TWIA Rules into General Knowledge**:  
    * *Temptation*: Spending exam time applying TWIA statutory declination rules on a general-knowledge question.  
    * *Resolving Fact*: General knowledge tests basic wind exclusions and the concept of residual coastal pools; specific TWIA statutory rules belong to Domain TX21. Supporting Rule ID: R-0011.

## **Simulator Seeds**

### **Seed 1**

> * **Blueprint Item**: A.2. HO-31  
> * **Tested Competency**: Identify peril coverage triggers for Coverage A (Dwelling) vs. Coverage C (Personal Property) under HO-38.  
> * **Fact-Pattern Concept**: A severe storm causes a tree branch to fall, breaking a window and damaging interior hardwood floors. Simultaneously, an expensive vase inside the house is knocked over and broken by the owner's dog fleeing the storm.  
> * **Correct Rule Summary**: Form HO-3 provides open-peril coverage on Coverage A (dwelling), covering the physical damage caused by the tree branch8. However, Coverage C (personal property) is restricted to broad named perils8. Accidental breakage of a vase caused by a domestic pet is not a broad named peril, so the vase is excluded8.  
> * **Plausible Distractor Logic**: Distractors will claim both losses are covered because HO-3 is "all-risk," or that neither is covered because wind-driven interior damage requires external roof penetration.  
> * **Difficulty**: Hard  
> * **Rule ID**: R-0028  
> * **Source Support**: ISO HO 00 03 05 11 Section I \- Perils Insured Against8.  
> * **Wording Risk**: Avoid using outdated "all-risk" wording; enforce "open perils" vs "named perils" terminology8.

### **Seed 2**

> * **Blueprint Item**: C.3. Businessowners Policy (BOP)1  
> * **Tested Competency**: Distinguish BOP Business Income duration and limits from CPP Commercial Property forms5.  
> * **Fact-Pattern Concept**: A small retail boutique insured under an unendorsed ISO Businessowners Policy (BP 00 03\) suffers a main fire loss, shutting down business completely for 9 months while rebuilding. The total lost net profit and continuing expenses equal $180,000.  
> * **Correct Rule Summary**: The unendorsed BOP automatically provides Business Income and Extra Expense coverage on an Actual Loss Sustained (ALS) basis for up to 12 consecutive months following the loss, without requiring a stated dollar limit of insurance on the policy Declarations6. The full $180,000 loss is covered.  
> * **Plausible Distractor Logic**: Distractors will state coverage is capped at $50,000 under a standard sublimit, or that a 50% coinsurance penalty applies due to lack of declared limits.  
> * **Difficulty**: Medium  
> * **Rule ID**: R-0096  
> * **Source Support**: ISO BP 00 03 07 13 Section I.A.5.f Business Income6.  
> * **Wording Risk**: Verify that question prompt specifies "unendorsed ISO BOP."

### **Seed 3**

> * **Blueprint Item**: E. National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)1  
> * **Tested Competency**: Apply statutory NFIP effective date waiting periods and loan closing exceptions18.  
> * **Fact-Pattern Concept**: A commercial property owner buys a flood insurance policy through NFIP Direct on March 1 for a building located in an SFHA, with no lender loan involved. On March 12, an overflow of inland waters floods the building.  
> * **Correct Rule Summary**: Voluntarily purchased NFIP flood policies are subject to a mandatory 30-day waiting period before taking effect18. Because the flood occurred on Day 11, no coverage applies.  
> * **Plausible Distractor Logic**: Distractors will suggest coverage takes effect in 24 hours, or 10 days, or immediately upon payment receipt.  
> * **Difficulty**: Medium  
> * **Rule ID**: R-01018  
> * **Source Support**: National Flood Insurance Act; FEMA NFIP Manual Section 218.  
> * **Wording Risk**: Clarify that the purchase was not connected to a loan origin/modification18.

### **Seed 4**

> * **Blueprint Item**: C.2.c. Business Income1  
> * **Tested Competency**: Calculate the 72-hour waiting period for Business Income under CP 00 305.  
> * **Fact-Pattern Concept**: A bakery suffers a direct explosion loss on Friday at 12:00 PM (noon), forcing complete business closure. Repairs are completed, and the bakery reopens the following Tuesday at 12:00 PM (noon). Daily lost net profit is $1,000.  
> * **Correct Rule Summary**: Business Income coverage under CP 00 30 incorporates a mandatory 72-hour waiting period that acts as a time deductible5. Coverage begins 72 hours after physical loss (Monday at 12:00 PM). The business was suspended for 4 total days (96 hours), but only 1 day (24 hours) falls after the 72-hour mark. Total payable Business Income is $1,000.  
> * **Plausible Distractor Logic**: Distractors will calculate full 4-day indemnity ($4,000) or claim zero coverage because closure was under 7 days.  
> * **Difficulty**: Hard  
> * **Rule ID**: R-0085  
> * **Source Support**: ISO CP 00 30 10 12 Section F.1 Period of Restoration5.  
> * **Wording Risk**: Ensure prompt explicitly specifies "CP 00 30 without time-deductible endorsement."
